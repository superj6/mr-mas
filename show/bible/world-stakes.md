## The answer in ten lines

1. **How much we have now: very little that lands on anyone.**
   - **China is scenery.** Mas has one China line in twelve episodes. The record gives him nine, and they change with the audience.
   - **The public nearly vanishes after Ep2.** From Ep3 on it's crowd noise, and it's gone by Ep8.
   - **No ordinary worker is ever on screen.** Mas's five real lines about jobs go unused.
   - **The economy is mostly numbers on props.** Energy barely appears.
2. **Why it matters:** the thriller's bomb is the calendar, and the world is its blast radius. Right now the stakes stop at the boardroom door.
3. **Every world beat answers one of three questions:** *who pays, who's asked, who can turn it off.* "Because China" is the answer everyone gives for not asking.
4. **One new character: DOT,** NopeAI's front-desk contractor. She's the consequence runner's hands from the locked pilot, and we finally see her face in Ep5. She is the one person the season never counts, until the machine hires her as its "human face."
5. **Three reused devices:**
   - **THE BLEACHERS:** the tiled crowds the show already draws, now carrying real poll numbers.
   - **THE QUESTION:** Ep2's `is it safe?`, which changes every time it comes back.
   - **THE PLUG:** the kill-switch cords, which turn out to run to the grid.
6. **One new prop, THE CHINA CARD:** a plain white card reading `CHINA` on one side and `AMERICA` on the other. It's played only where the record shows the speaker citing China.
7. **Delivery:** everything arrives where Mas already lives (the lobby, the dark-room monitor, his phone, the roof, the carousel window) and lands on a face within 10 s. No V.O. is added, and there's no Mas tell at a real event.
8. **Budget:** 77 core insertions across twelve episodes. The net runtime change is ≤ 0 in every episode, and Ep5 comes out 17 s short. Every added second is paid for with a cut in the same episode.
9. **Mas's share of screen time goes up.** The world enters through his rooms and his screens, not through new exits from his POV.
10. **Before lock:** 57 new timeline rows and 11 corrections (§7), and six sign-offs (§9). The biggest is the showrunner's approval of a new speaking character.

### 1.2 The blast radius

**The tone guide's bomb is the calendar** ([§1.1](tone-and-dialogue.md#11-the-statement)). The audience knows the headlines, so the suspense lives in *how things happen, what they cost, and whom*. At the moment the cost stops at the boardroom door: who runs NopeAI, who's in the photo, whose valuation passes whose.

The four threads are the blast radius. They make the danger feel real and on a clock, as the tone guide asks:

- **The race has a second runner, and a mirror.** Every "because China" in Washington has an answer across the water: a magnet on the tollbooth arm, a customs gate, a second table. Each side's lever turns out to be the other side's hostage. That's thriller escalation the record already supplies.
- **The build-out has a bill, and the bill has an address.** Gigawatts become electric bills, federal loans and a pledge to pay "whether they use the electricity or not." A plug that nobody can pull is the endgame's real mechanism.
- **The machine takes a job before it takes a seat.** Before THE INTERN gets its desk, a contractor installs the plate. Before the model runs the dinner, it learns its cheer from the front desk.
- **The public wants what Mas wants: to be asked.** About six in ten Americans want more control over how AI is used in their lives [V✓]. Nobody asks them, and nobody asks him. In Ep12 both RSVPs auto-accept.

This also deepens the theme ([overview §3](overview.md#3-theme)): *everyone is building their own successor.* Dot records the greeting that will replace her. The public trains the model with its questions. Each government builds the thing it fears the other has.

## 3. DOT: the everyday character

### 3.1 The pitch

> **DOT** has been changing Mas's signs since the first episode. We've only ever seen her hands. She's the contractor at NopeAI's front desk, with an orange badge in a building of teal ones. She badges him in every morning. She asked the product its first question in the building, and she grades its answers at night. She spends the season installing the machine's desk, recording the greeting it will use and handing it her screwdriver. The only thing she ever wanted was a teal badge. In the finale she gets one: the machine issues it, and it reads `HUMAN FACE`.

### 3.3 Why her

- **She already exists in the locked pilot.** `cast.hands.worker` is the consequence runner (tone guide §4, tool 8): the box of spare `0` plates, and the four screws on ALYI's nameplate. Giving those hands a face is a reveal that reframes what we've seen, which is the *Mr. Robot* move, and it costs almost nothing.
- **She lives in Mas's home room.** The lobby is where he passes every episode, so every Dot beat sits inside his POV.
- **Her job is the one the machine takes first:** front desk, help line and answer-grading. That's exactly the record's pattern: customer support, entry-level hiring, and paying workers to train their replacements.
- **She joins two threads.** She's a worker, and she's a user who uses more and trusts less: the first question in Ep1, "It's free." in Ep4, the Cancel click in Ep7, "Is it safe?" in Ep10.
- **She mirrors THE INTERN.** Two lanyards reprint all season. The Intern's goes up; hers goes sideways, and then the machine issues it.
- **Her ending is the thesis.** The veto says "people prefer a human face." Dot is the human face the machine actually hires.

### 3.6 Rules for writing her

- **Invented.** She is not a composite of any named or real worker. No home life, family, dating or address (X1). Her life off shift never appears.
- **Never the butt, never a halo.** Every joke about her lands on institutions or on Mas. Her own jokes are dry wins. She's mercenary (the soup), complicit (the training videos) and right.
- **Never in distress for a laugh.** Losing the screwdriver is played with a still face and a blank lanyard. No tears, no pleading, no pity staging.
- **At most one Dot beat per episode**, except Eps 5, 9 and 12, which carry her scenes. Most of her beats are 0-s recasts of figures already in the drafts.
- **She never explains the theme.** She never says "replaced," "counted" or "human." The lanyard and the casing carry it.
- **Terminology:** "intern" means the machine only. Rename the reel's human `intern` ids (1.06, 1.32, 1.40) to `staffer`, as SEASON-NOTES asks. Dot is "contract," never "intern."

### 3.7 Cost and sign-off

- **Assets:** Eps 1–4 reuse `cast.hands.worker` with an orange cuff and headset audio. Ep5 adds one portrait with three expressions (bright, flat, reading). The lobby-at-night plate is a palette step of `room.lobby`.
- **Lines and performer:** about 14 lines across the season, for one performer. Her voice is human, cartoon register, never cloned ([guardrails §5](guardrails.md#5-legal-hygiene)).
- **The Ep1 Act Four production files don't change.** The optional `GUEST`-badge hand runs only if Act Four reopens.

## 5. Delivering the world inside Mas's POV

**The rules:**
1. **The world comes to him.** Every world beat plays in a room he's in, or through his screen, window or phone, with his glass or hand in frame ([POV §1.5](pov-and-framing.md#15-how-much-of-the-show-is-his)). None of the core items is an exit from his POV.
2. **The lobby is the world's door.** NopeAI's lobby is a home room he crosses every episode: Dot at the desk, the TV in its own medium, the revolving door. Real items play on the TV. Dot and Mas play in front of it.
3. **Macro plays on the dark-room monitor.** China, markets and policy run there as one-surface runs, and the rail rolls like the odometer (tone guide §4, tool 12).
4. **The far bank is across the water.** China's side is always seen from the pier, the roof or the carousel window. It stays at scenery distance and never becomes a set.
5. **Real items walk in dry** (tone guide §5): dated as they land, in their own medium, word for word, and on a face within 10 s. That face belongs to someone whose plan the item breaks, usually Dot, a Bleachers tile, ZURC, NESNEJ or Mario.
6. **Mas's own real lines land on a prop consequence.** "Tasks, not jobs" lands on the clone reading the senator's card. The lamplighter line lands on Dot's bulb. "Delighted to be wrong" lands on "Take two." None of them lands on the Orb's look.
7. **No V.O. near a world item, and no Mas tell at a real one.** His tells go on Dot scenes, which are invented and at least a bar away. That's where he gets close: the late beat in Ep5, and his stillness at the glass in Ep9.
8. **His China arc is carried by objects only:** the poster, the op-ed, the pier crate, the memo, the dais card, the letter, the chair. He states no motive at a real event, ever.
9. **One number per beat.** If the picture carries the fact, the rail carries only the date and the tag. The poll numbers ride on THE BLEACHERS, not in chyron stacks.
10. **The act-outs get a new kind of turn.** The tone guide asks us to rotate turns ([§3 #10](tone-and-dialogue.md#3-clunky-patterns-to-eliminate)), including "the world changing." The balanced tollbooth arm, the odometer running backward and the Bay going dark give act-outs shapes the season hasn't used.
