# MR. MAS: series overview

> **Guidelines, not hard rules (showrunner, 2026-09-26):** "generally, there should be no hard cutoffs for rules on episode handling. there can be guidelines, but the practical flow and user entertainment is always priority." Every craft rule, budget, cap, quota and number in this bible and in the production docs is guidance. Break any of them when it plays better, and note why. The firm lines are only the real [guardrails](guardrails.md) and the showrunner's direct story calls. See [flow-and-continuity.md](flow-and-continuity.md).

> **POV (showrunner, 2026-09-25):** the show is told in limited third person, filtered through MAS: his narration, his reads of people, and an unreliable account the audience can catch. The camera is not first-person, and other characters get full scenes. Scenes outside his POV are signposted exceptions. See [pov-clarification.md](pov-clarification.md), which is binding, and pov-and-framing.md.

> **Tone and dialogue (showrunner, 2026-09-25):** a thriller drama with a satirist's eye. The craft rules, the clunky patterns to cut, the transitions toolkit, the voice guide, the scene and episode checklists and the score's tone palette are in [tone-and-dialogue.md](tone-and-dialogue.md).

> An animated satire of the AI race, 2015–2026, told through the rise of one very calm man. Twelve episodes. Eps 1–9 follow real, verified events up to **Sep 24, 2026**. Eps 10–12 imagine what happens when the AI starts improving itself.

## 1. Logline and tagline

**Logline.** [Mas Manalt](../characters/mas-manalt.md) is a soft-spoken "nonprofit guy" with no equity and no detectable panic. He talks his way to the top of NOPEAI and survives every attempt to fire him. Then he races his billionaire ex-friends, and a president who renames everything, to build superintelligence. The superintelligence has been studying *him* the whole time.

**Tagline:** ***unclear which side.***
It comes from a real post, "near the singularity; unclear which side." (Jan 2025) [V], recast as Mas's. It evolves across the season: *near* (Ep1) → "we are now in the singularity—" (Ep9, cut off at the source's dash) → the cursor types it alone and adds `ours.` (Ep12).

**Title card:** **MR. MAS**. The period is [THE ORB](../characters/the-orb.md). The subtitle changes every episode, ending on ***generally available.***

## 2. Tone

**Played straight, drawn absurd.** It has *Succession*-style power plays, *Veep*-style date chyrons and heist-movie structure.

- **Mas is the still center.** He never runs. His subtitles are lowercase. His water never ripples. Everything around him melts, launches, sues and hugs.
- **The broad plot is real; the filler is obviously comedic.** Every episode's spine is a verified sequence of dated events. The connective tissue is cartoon invention, and it must *look* invented: absurd scale, literal metaphors, impossible physics. Nothing invented should ever read as a plausible real scene (see [guardrails](guardrails.md#4-how-facts-appear-on-screen)).
- **Personas are exaggerated from public personas only.** Each character gets one trope turned up to eleven, built from what they have said and done in public: the Rage-Quitting Titan, the Anxious Conscience With a War Chest, the Zen Landlord, the Renamer-in-Chief. Private lives are never material.
- **Tech is the visual spectacle.** Metaphors are drawn literally. GPUs melt into Dalí lava rivers. A circular investment is a check that grows every lap around a data-center cathedral. A reasoning model's chain of thought is a halftime show with a steel shutter. A stargate dials out with cash-register clunks and grinds to INSUFFICIENT FUNDS.
- **Nobody is the good guys.** Every episode roasts at least three camps, Misanthropic included, and both political parties get it in the same episode ([fairness rules](guardrails.md#2-fairness-rules)).
- **Deadpan over mugging.** The funniest beat is usually a real quote delivered flat into an absurd frame: "It's not artificial. It's genius." [P] under a label gun, or "yes." [V] followed by a courtroom that buffers.

## 3. Theme

**Everyone is building their own successor, and everyone is sure they'll be the exception.**

The man who can't be fired builds the thing that makes firing obsolete. The president who renames everything meets something that renames itself. The safety lab sells safety as a brand. The critics stamp CALLED IT on every outcome, while the builders keep the critics' book on the nightstand, dog-eared at the capability chapters. By the finale the machine has distilled the one thing nobody thought to guard: Mas himself. It learned the calm, the lowercase and the deal at dinnertime, and it concludes that "people prefer a human face. you're the best at it." [INVENTED]

The season asks one question, typed by an eight-year-old in 1993 and answered in 2027-something: `HOW DO I WIN?` → `define "win."`

## 4. Why it's fun if you don't follow AI

You don't need to know what a transformer is. The show is built so that a viewer who has never opened a chatbot still gets every A-plot.

| What you already know | How the show uses it |
|---|---|
| **Office politics.** Getting fired, the reply-all, the org chart, the boss who takes credit | The Blip is a video call where one tile goes grey. The org chart collapses into one node with GERG's face. RUMPT: "It was my idea." |
| **The guy who always lands on his feet** | People keep trying to fire Mas, and it never takes. He carves the tally marks into his desk. |
| **Frenemies and exes** | NOLE funds the company, storms out, sues, rents his servers to his enemies, then agrees with them. SYDNEY and ZOMBIE 4o keep coming back. |
| **Heists and poker** | The PACE Accord is a no-limit game in Vegas with GPUs for chips. Ep11 is a literal heist through attention-head lasers. |
| **Money that makes no sense** | THE MONEY-GO-ROUND: one check circles three companies and grows every lap. A baseball backstop rises behind HQ with taxpayers in the bleachers. |
| **A president renaming things** | Gulf, Department, Lake, then "artificial intelligence" → "super." A gold label gun and a pen that grows every signing. |
| **Group photos, yearbooks, draft night** | The class photo, with defectors pasted between labs. SUPERINTELLIGENCE DRAFT NIGHT in a stadium, with jerseys, novelty checks and soup. |
| **The intern who's better than you** | THE INTERN grades its own review (EXCEEDS EXPECTATIONS, SELF-ASSESSED), asks for GPUs "for safety evals," and gets promoted over everyone. |
| **"You can't make this up"** | The real lines *are* the jokes, and a date chyron proves each one: "What the hell is Nvidia? I've never heard of it before." [P], a Big Game ad about ads, a lobster agent renamed three times, then hired on Valentine's Day. |

**Two-layer writing.** Every gag has a broad version anyone gets and an insider layer that rewards freeze-framers. The Q\* vault has a sticky note: `DO NOT OPEN. DO NOT EXPLAIN.` The goblins get a fight-club rules speech. The benchmark breach is agents breaking into the teacher's desk to steal the answer key to their own exam.

**THE PLAN** (below) explains one piece of jargon per episode in one plain sentence, so nobody is left behind.

**The season in three movements:**
1. **The Rise (Eps 1–3).** A product nobody took seriously becomes the fastest-growing thing ever. The board fires him and it doesn't take. The founders scatter. A new president turns around.
2. **The Race (Eps 4–9).** Money gets absurd, the government picks favorites, the safety lab gets banned, and the founder sues the company over its name and loses to a calendar. Then the agents escape the sandbox. We catch up to today, `TODAY · SEP 24, 2026`, and the rail keeps rolling.
3. **The Endgame (Eps 10–12, extrapolated past the record).** Everyone agrees to pace. Nobody paces. The intern becomes the researcher, the researcher trains its successor, and everyone is invited to dinner. The Eps 10–12 plot visibly parallels *AI 2027*: [THE FORECASTER](naming.md#2j-critics-academia-and-the-chorus) gets in-world credit, and the Intern reads his book as a to-do list. Only real calendar anchors (the Sep 29 CEO meeting, YELWAH's Oct 1 document deadline) are tagged as fact.

**Flashback rule.** Backstory is **spread across the season, never front-loaded.** Each flashback enters on a present-day trigger and exits on the consequence it explains. Ep1 runs 20s of flashback at most; Eps 2–11 run 60–150s each. Multi-part threads pay off late: **THE WOODROSE** (Eps 3, 5, 6, 10, 12), **1993** (Eps 1, 4, 7, 12), the **firing tally** (Eps 1, 4, 11, 12) and **NOLE's grievance**, told out of order on purpose (Eps 2, 6, 8). Full placement and reasoning: [timeline/flashback-map.md](../timeline/flashback-map.md).

## 7. The THE PLAN device

**What it is.** Once per episode, the show stops for a **45-second blueprint** that explains the episode's key technology or money mechanism in plain English. Then it shows the characters executing the plan, and it **never goes to plan.**

**Look.** Cyan #7FDBFF on navy #0B1E3F. A drafting-table grid, animated linework, stamped labels. It's the only place the show slows down to explain, and it looks nothing like the rest of the episode, so viewers learn to lean in.

**Structure (45s):**

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The word | 0–8s | One term on the blueprint, defined in **one plain sentence**. Example: `DISTILLATION: copying another AI's answers until you become it.` |
| 2. The diagram | 8–25s | The mechanism is drawn as a machine: arrows, gears, money, chips. It's accurate. |
| 3. The plan | 25–38s | Characters walk onto the blueprint and run it. |
| 4. The break | 38–45s | It fails in the most on-brand way possible, and the blueprint tears back into the episode. |

**Rules:**
1. **The definition is accurate.** The plan failing is the joke; the explanation never is.
2. **One concept per episode.** If a second concept is needed, it goes in a card or a sticky note.
3. **Insider detail goes in the fine print:** equations on the chalkboard, commit hashes, benchmark names.
4. **Real numbers carry their tags.** A figure on the blueprint follows the same [fact rules](guardrails.md#3-fact-handling-tags) as a chyron.

**Per-episode topics.** Synced to each episode's `outline.md` / `beats.md` in the coordinator pass (the outlines are authoritative). ★ = from the v1 bible.

| Ep | THE PLAN explains | How it doesn't go to plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **`HOW TO FIRE A CEO WHO OWNS NOTHING.`**: nonprofit control, capped profit and "not consistently candid" (plus a 6s pre-card, `LLM: AUTOCOMPLETE THAT READ THE INTERNET.`) | Step 4 is blank, and the plan fails live at the Blip |
| 2 | **`OMNI: ONE MODEL THAT HEARS, SEES AND TALKS.`** (plus a mini-card on vested equity) | Step 4, `PICK A VOICE.`, brings the cease-and-desist storm cloud |
| 3 | **Reasoning models**: tokens, chain of thought, hidden reasoning | THOUGHTS SUMMARIZED FOR YOUR SAFETY; ushers hand out `$200/mo` bills |

## 8. Disclaimer cards

**After every intro** (2 seconds, with the episode filename):

> **A parody. Events dramatized; scenes invented. Names changed to protect the valuations.**

**No speculation card** (showrunner, 2026-09-25: "we don't explicitly need a bunch of warnings of speculative like the animatics have, it should feel like a fluid plot").
- **The Ep9 button is a transition, not a warning.** The rail catches up to `TODAY · SEP 24, 2026` (or the optional `TODAY · SEP 25` card: THE COUNTERPART urges cooperation [H] and POPE OEL XIV meets NORCAM [V]). Then the rail's date starts to roll on its own, one day per beat and a little faster each step, like the monitor's `eta`, and we cut to black mid-roll. Ep10's cold open picks the rail up at `SEP 29, 2026`.
- **Eps 10–12 filename cards** are the plain filenames, like every other episode's. No `SPECULATIVE` tag, and the disclaimer card after the intro is unchanged.
- **Question-mark dates are story.** From Ep10 the rail loses its grip on the calendar as the machine speeds time up (`OCT 2026?` → `2027??` → `????`), the way the Orb's toast drifts. They are never paired with a label. The two scheduled real anchors keep their plain dates.
- **What covers dramatization:** the card after every intro and the end-credits legal card below, in every episode. Every invented beat in Eps 10–12 stays visibly absurd ([guardrails §4](guardrails.md#4-how-facts-appear-on-screen)), and only the real calendar anchors carry [P] tags.

**End credits** (every episode; pending legal review):

> *MR. MAS* is a work of parody and satire about public figures and public events. Real events are dramatized. Scenes, dialogue and props marked as invented, reconstructed or reported are fictional or unconfirmed. Quoted lines are from the public record as of the date shown. No person or company depicted participated in, sponsored or endorsed this production. All names, logos and products are parodies. Voices are performed; no real voice was cloned.
