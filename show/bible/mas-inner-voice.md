## 1. What it's for

1. **Attachment.**
   - The audience should know what he's thinking, often enough to be on his side of the glass.
   - He's calm outside because he's busy inside. The calm is a skill (the ALL-CAPS kid learned lowercase), and the voice is where we watch him do it.
2. **Orientation without pointers** (SHOWRUNNER-NOTES 3).
   - His read of a room tells a newcomer who these people are and what each one wants. That's the job the lower-thirds, side badges and hedge labels were doing.
3. **Suspense by trust.**
   - His reads of people are right, again and again, so we come to rely on them.
   - The one he gets wrong (the Friday call, Act Four) hurts because we believed him too.

## 2. Who he's talking to, and when

- **Himself, in the moment, present tense.**
  - He doesn't know what's coming, so the voice can't leak the firing.
  - It goes quiet when the blow lands, and we're surprised with him.
- **A silent addressee: the 1993 kid.**
  - The voice is his running answer to the question on that screen (`HOW DO I WIN?`, seen in Ep12).
  - It never says "you" or "kid" and never mentions 1993. It's a reason for the voice to exist, and a loneliness under it.
  - The season pays it off: in Ep12 we see the question he's been answering all along.
- **Not the viewer.** He performs for everyone already. Speaking to us would be one more performance.
- **Not the Orb.** The Orb is the witness who can catch him (the one true word aloud, "mostly."). It stays that.

## 3. What he thinks about (four kinds, most to least)

1. **Reads of other people.**
   - Checkable details: a count, a name, a prop, a timing, what someone wants.
   - *"gerg wants to ship it. rima wants it quiet. alyi wants to know what it is first."*
2. **Predictions the picture pays off.**
   - *"she'll go for three."*, and she underlines it a third time.
   - *"gerg. he'll say he's compiling."*, and Gerg says it.
   - Two or three per episode earn the trust §1.3 spends.
3. **Warmth.**
   - Who someone is to him, in one plain line.
   - *"alyi asks that about everything we build. he means it every time."*
4. **The gap:** the honest thought, then the line he says. Never explain the gap.
   - *"she's right. it will break. i don't know which part yet."* → aloud: "it's a preview."
   - "it's the bill." → *"mostly the bill."*

**Rhythm carries what he won't say.** He never says he feels anything. When he's rattled, a count speeds up, restarts or corrects itself:
- *"four hundred and six. four hundred and seven. four hundred and six."*

## 4. Rules

1. **Read other people more than himself.** He's reliable about them and unreliable about himself.
2. **Mostly true.** At most a quarter of his lines are "caught" by the picture. A voice that's always caught gets laughed *at*, and that's distance.
3. **Humour from precision, never from punchline shapes.** If the line would work as a post, cut it: posts are his public register.
4. **No puns, no aphorisms, no life lessons,** no "little did i know", no "you're probably wondering".
5. **Never describe the picture.** Strip the V.O. out, and the scene should still play; with it, we know him.
6. **No jargon and no explaining.** If he starts to explain something, he drops it for the human read.
7. **No winks at the real-world news.** He doesn't know he's in a parody, and he doesn't know how it ends.
8. **Cluster, don't sprinkle.** 3–6 lines in a room he's reading, then none for a while.
   - In those rooms his inner words can outnumber his spoken ones, as in the Dexter pilot.
9. **Go silent at the big moments.** We've heard him all episode, so the silence is loud. (Taxi Driver, American Psycho, Fleabag's finale, House of Cards' opener.)
10. **Lowercase, plain, short sentences,** with full stops. Length is whatever the thought needs, usually 3–12 words and rarely two sentences over 15.
11. **Once an episode, the effort shows:** a private rehearsal, a line tried twice, "super." chosen from three.
12. **Never on the board's side.** When we don't hear him, we aren't with him. That's how the told-twice structure reads without badges.

## 5. Where he's silent

- **The blow:** from the call's first tile until "super.". Nothing inside him that we hear.
- **At contested or credibility moments:** the reasons for the firing, testimony, the NDA line, Ep7's HUG and the Pentagon deal, the sealed memo (guardrails §6; pov-and-framing §5.5).
- **In government proceedings** (Senate, UN, Security Council), unless it's a read of someone else's small tell on an invented beat.
- **Over another character's sincere beat or punchline.**
- **On another person's real act that hurts him:** "alyi voted." / "He did both." get the held face and the room, nothing inside.
- **In the cold open and the intro.** The cold open's line is spoken, on camera.
- **Scenes told from someone else's side.**

## 6. Guardrails (unchanged in substance)

- **Never a motive at a contested real moment.** At a real but uncontested moment he may notice, count, be amused, be curious or feel warmth, since none of that makes a claim about the record. The launch night, the hearts and the lobby are like that.
- **Feelings are fine on invented beats:** the chatbot flattering him, the badge, Gerg's call, the carve. A feeling about someone else's real act is not.
- **Never private life** (X1), **never clinical** (X3), never a hesitation on a credibility question.
- **Never re-voice a real quote as a thought.** Real lines stay spoken or posted, verbatim.
- **Facts inside a thought still have to be true.** The "race weekend" in Vegas (Nov 17, 2023) gets a facts-file row before it's used on screen.
- **The lint** (pov-and-framing §5.8) still flags *knew, planned, decided, wanted, meant, realized, because, so that, on purpose, strategy, candid, honest, quiet, sincere* at a contested beat.

## 7. How much

| | Ep1 v2 | v3 target | Reference points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lines | 3 | **15–25**, in 4–6 clusters | Mr. Robot pilot 53 · Dexter pilot 75 · Taxi Driver 36 (in 2 h) |
| Words | 9 | **200–300** | American Psycho ≈ 650 (in 100 min) · Mr. Robot pilot ≈ 2,200 |
| Share of runtime | under 1% | about 6–9% | Mr. Robot / Dexter / Wolf 22–26% of dialogue |

Eps 10–12 thin it again as the machine's point of view grows (pov-and-framing §5.4's season curve holds). **The Ep12 turn:** the machine answers the question his voice has been answering.

## 8. Is it corny? (read each line against this)

- Would it work as a post, a tagline or a fridge magnet? **Cut.**
- Does it tell us what we just saw? **Cut.**
- Does it name a feeling ("i felt", "it hurt")? **Rewrite** as a count, a read or silence.
- Does it wink at the real events? **Cut.**
- Is it the third caught line in a row? **Make one true.**
- Could a newcomer follow the scene better with it? **Keep**, if it passes the four above.
- Is the moment bigger than any line he could think? **Silence.**

## 9. Performance and screen

- **Voice:** the same performer as his speech (a stock synthetic voice until the part is cast; never a clone). Close, dry and unhurried, about 110–130 wpm, slower than he talks to people.
- **Screen:** one lowercase line in his cyan, above the band, typed at his rate (pov-and-framing §5.2). It never shares the screen with a rail item or a toast.
