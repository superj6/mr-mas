## The approach in ten lines

1. **Limited third person, close.** The camera sits beside Mas, not across the room. Faces and hands fill at least 55% of story time, and room wides at most 15%.
2. **There are four versions of Mas.** To the room he speaks, in eight words or fewer. To us he gives lowercase V.O. To THE ORB he gives one true word. To no one he shows hands, props and rituals. Only the audience gets all four.
3. **The V.O. is sparing:** 5–8 lines an episode (pilot ≤ 8), 60 words at most, in at least three of the four movements. There is more of it when he's cornered, and none near the record. **Nobody in the world ever hears it**, the Orb included.
4. **He is unreliable in tone, never in fact.** The rail and the Orb are never wrong, and every distortion is corrected on screen within a bar. In a told-twice structure, the other side's pass outranks his on invented beats.
5. **The toolkit has eight devices,** each with a cap: at most 6 caught per episode and 2 per scene. The V.O. sets up and the picture delivers.
6. **His inner life comes from his public persona.** He wants to be asked, not just to survive. He fears Cancel working, and a calm that can be copied. His tells are props, and nothing is clinical.
7. **A new framing grammar:**
   - a medium and two-shot tier for principals in home rooms
   - the portrait fallaway as the cheap intimate shot
   - one full-frame close-up for Mas
   - an insert kit for his hands and eyes
   - two-shot first
   - the listener
   - everyone gets one real face
8. **The adventure game is a signature, used sparingly.** The wide is the map, and lit UI appears at most twice an episode. The lit cursor is the player's seat, not Mas; the unlit arrow belongs to whoever is trying to fire him. He knows he's being played ("noted.").
9. **Exits from his POV are signposted.** The rail names the side, there's no V.O., and he appears only as his public record (or as his voice on their call). Exits take at most 12% of story time. We come back through the glass.
10. **The cost is about neutral:** about 10 agent-h one-time for the season, and about −0.5 agent-h per episode. Build THE FALLING TILE and 26A in this grammar for the W0 calibration now.

## 1. The POV contract

### 1.1 Whose story

The story is limited third person, told close. The camera stands beside Mas, not across the room, and it's free: other characters get full scenes, close-ups and quiet beats of their own. What makes it his story is what each scene is **about**:
- what he notices
- how he reads people (the poker player's "patterns in people over time" [K])
- what he chooses to say about it
- what his hands do while his face doesn't

**The contract line:** ***we know what he knows about the world when he does. We don't know his next move. We see more of him than he shows.***
- He learns the news when we do: the rail, the call, the post. He can read the rail and answer it.
- What he'll do about it is his. He knows the deal before dinner; we find out at dinner.
- The audience sees the tells, the rituals and the gap between what he says and what the picture shows.
- Nobody, including us, ever learns his reasons at a real event. That is the guardrail, and it is also the theme: we learn Mas from his inputs, the way the model does (Ep12).

*(Revised after the table read. The earlier line, "he's ahead of us on the plot; we're ahead of him on himself," contradicted the binding clarification's "we mostly know what he knows." The character file [mas-manalt.md](../characters/mas-manalt.md) takes the same wording; that's its owner's handoff.)*

**What we borrow from the reference shows**:

| Show | What we borrow |
|---|---|
| *Dexter* | The composed-mask V.O. set against the reality |
| *Fleabag* | The camera as confidant. What he hides from us matters. |
| *House of Cards* | Complicity as a single glance, never a speech |
| *The Wolf of Wall Street* | Narration the picture corrects. Here the Orb's rewind does the correcting. |
| *Better Call Saul*, *Barry* | Anchored on one lead, with signposted scenes from the other side |
| *Kaguya-sama* | A calm surface over a scheming interior, but only about tiny invented stakes |
| *I, Tonya*, *Rashomon* | Competing accounts: the Blip told twice, the Ep8 Rashomon |

From *Mr. Robot* we take the reveal structure only, never the psychology.

### 1.2 Four layers, one order of precedence

| Layer | Whose | Where it lives | Can it be wrong? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THE RECORD** | The show | The rail (the dimmed band), dated quote cards, truth labels, THE PLAN, chyron runs | **Never.** It is true to its tag. |
| **THE WITNESS** | [THE ORB](../characters/the-orb.md), "the show's one objective narrator" | Toasts, its iris and gaze, and the iris replay from Ep6 | **Never wrong about Mas.** It never flatters and never gives an opinion: only a look, a toast or a replay. It may hedge (`human (probably)`, Ep6), go silent or return nothing (Ep10), or answer `SIDE: UNCLEAR` (Ep12), but it never gives a wrong verdict. It reacts to what it can see (objects, his hands, his face), **never to the narration.** |
| **HIM** | Mas | What the camera attends to; his V.O.; his spoken lines; MAS'S VERSION shots | **Yes, in tone only** (§2.1). |
| **THE MACHINE** | The model-to-be | GLYPH, the suggested-replies strip, TERMINAL, the lit cursor (§1.6) | It isn't narrating; it's reading him. He never sees GLYPH. He always takes the strip's offer, until Ep12's three empty bubbles. |

**The record outranks the witness, and the witness outranks him.** When his account disagrees with the rail or the Orb, the rail and the Orb are right, and the picture shows it within a bar.

- **The other side outranks him on invented beats.** In a told-twice structure, THE OTHER SIDE (a signposted exit, §6) is right wherever the two passes disagree about an invented beat. On real events, both passes agree on every tagged fact. *(Ep1: the board's pass shows that nobody looked up at "super."; on his screen four faces froze, because his feed froze on one bar of Wi-Fi.)*
- **The witness has limits, not errors.** From Ep10 the rail and the picture correct him alone, as the Orb goes quiet. Its last `HUMAN: VERIFIED.` in Ep12 is the witness answering F12.1 (§3.3).

### 1.3 The four versions of Mas

| Version | Who hears it | Form | Rule | Ep1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **To the room** | Everyone present | Spoken, lowercase, ≤ 8 words | The public face at its minimum: the "one calm" of pacing law L4 | "super." into the still-open mic (sc 26) |
| **To us** | The viewer | V.O. (§5) | Gracious, small and technically true | "the meeting ended early." (26A) |
| **To the Orb** | The machine | One word or a qualifier (≤ 3 words), spoken aloud in the dark room, answering the Orb's look at the thing his own account just shrank. **≤ 1 per episode.** Other lines he says aloud in the dark room ("you can stay.", "close.") are speech to the room: stage them to the room or an object, never as the Orb's qualifier | The nearest he comes to confessing. A chime or a cut always undercuts it. | "mostly." (sc 29: the badge was a joke, mostly) |
| **To no one** | Nobody | Hands, props, rituals, the glass | Never spoken. The truest version. | He carves mark 3 with the pen he pocketed and rests his thumb on it (sc 26A); the drawer and the framed lanyard (sc 32) |

**Only the audience gets all four,** and that is where the attachment comes from. We are the one audience he doesn't fully perform for, and even for us he performs a little. **The V.O. is never heard in the world**: no character, the Orb and the model included, hears it, records it or replays it. It stays ours. He is most honest with the machine out loud, which is how the machine learns him: the Ep9 replay of "mostly.", and Ep12's "you're the best at it."

### 1.4 Who knows what

| | The audience | Mas |
|---|---|---|
| The rail: dates, numbers, truth labels | Sees it | Sees it. He can glance at it (≤ 2 per episode) and answer it ("noted.") |
| His V.O. | Hears it | It's his account, told to us. Nobody in the world hears it |
| GLYPH | Sees it | Never. He can react to the event it rides on, never to the tokens. |
| The suggested-replies strip | Sees it | Sees it, and takes the offer |
| The other side's pass (signposted exits) | Sees it | Is never shown knowing what happened there |
| His hands, the drawer, the tally | Sees them | Knows them; never mentions them |
| His feelings | Infers them from the Orb and no-one versions | Never confesses them |
| His reasons at real events | Never learns them | Never tells |

### 1.5 How much of the show is his

| Share of story time | Target | Pilot (projected) |
|---|---|---|
| **His POV:** he's present, or we're looking through his screen, window or phone | **≥ 70% in every episode, Eps 10–12 included** | ≈ 82% |
| **The show's voice:** THE PLAN, quote cards, act-out cards, chyron runs | ≤ 15% | ≈ 9% |
| **Signposted exits** (§6) | ≤ 12%, ≤ 3 per episode | ≈ 9% (pass one, 1:50) |
| **Inside him:** V.O. plus the picture devices in §2 | ≤ 45 s per episode | ≈ 30 s |

**Shoot it before you say it.** The camera does most of the interior work, and the V.O. is seasoning. If an eyeline and an insert can carry a beat, don't write a line. If the picture already has the laugh, cut the line (Ep1 sc 26).

**"Primarily his POV" has to survive the finale.** Eps 10–12 hold the 70% too: the machine grows *inside* his view (the strip suggesting replies before he opens the app, replays on his monitor, the Intern in his eyelines), and TERMINAL scenes stay at about 15% of story time or less. *(The Eps 10–12 owners confirm.)*

### 1.6 The cursor is not his

This is the ruling on the adventure-game layer.
- **The lit cursor and its sentence line** (`Look at glass of water`) belong to *the player's seat*, not to Mas.
  - In the pilot it is the viewer's seat.
  - The season reveals who else has been sitting in it: in Ep12 the cursor types `ours.` on its own ([overview §1](overview.md#1-logline-and-tagline)).
- **Mas can see the interface.** He reads the rail and answers the cursor ("noted.", sc 33), but he never drives it.
- **Pointers that click on him belong to other people.** There are two, and they never mix:
  - **The lit cursor** (the player's seat) looks and asks. It never moves his body and never picks his words.
  - **The unlit arrow** belongs to whoever is trying to fire him: the stranger in 1993, the board (it clicks Cancel in sc 26, and the same arrow bonks on the greyed Cancel in sc 30), and the Ep12 vote.
- **When an episode needs him to choose,** the choice is the phone's suggestion strip, grown to three lines if needed (§4.5). Which option he takes is the tell. The cursor never makes it for him. *(Ep2 sc 14's dialogue tree is redrawn this way: its owner's handoff.)*

This keeps the canon, keeps Mas from becoming a gamer, and gives the rare lit-UI moment its meaning: *he knows he's being played.*

## 2. The unreliable-narrator toolkit

### 2.1 The principle: wrong in tone, never in fact

Mas is a composed, clear-headed narrator. He is never confused, deluded or split (guardrail X3). His account is unreliable the way a good press answer is: **every line survives a fact-check, and no line survives the picture.**

| His account may | His account may never |
|---|---|
| Understate scale | State a wrong date, number or name, or get wrong who did what |
| Relabel an event with a softer word | State a motive, an intention, or what he knew at a real event |
| Leave something out | Answer, dispute or comment on a real accusation. "Not consistently candid" is never answered, in any episode. |
| Notice the wrong detail | Touch `(REPORTED)`, `(DISPUTED)`, sealed or contested material, even with a look |
| Be gracious while someone is pushed aside | Paraphrase, trim or voice a real line. Real lines stay on camera or on cards. |
| Deny a feeling | Claim a feeling ("i was hurt." is never written) |
| Flatter his own behaviour in an invented beat (D5) | Flatter, restage or "correct" the substance of a contested real event |

### 2.2 The correction clock

- **Every distortion is corrected on screen within 1 bar of the line ending.** The hard limit is 4 bars. This is pacing law L2 applied to narration.
- **The correction comes from the record, the witness or the picture.** The V.O. goes first and the correction follows on the next beat: his word, then the record's.
- **The correction may itself be a real item** (the rail's `+1 FIRING`, a real post). The distortion never overlaps it.
- **One long-range payoff per episode is allowed.** In the pilot, 26A's "i don't keep score." pays off at the tag's drawer. Even that line gets an immediate contradiction in the same shot (the tally he just carved) and the next (the Orb counts it).
- **Told twice.** In a told-twice structure, the other pass may be the correction at any distance, as long as the rail labels both passes (§6.1).

**Standing devices** (already canon):
- **The look.**
  - At most 1 look into the lens per episode: 1 beat, no change of expression, only on an invented beat about himself, never within 2 bars of a real line or card.
  - At most 2 glances at the rail.
  - The pilot has already spent all three: the lens in sc 13 (the class photo), the rail in sc 2 and sc 23. **So Act Four adds no look.**
- **The late beat** (§3.4).
- **The silence.** This is a rule, not a device.
  - The V.O. never comes within 1 bar of a real line, a dated quote card, a name card, or a `(REPORTED)`, `(DISPUTED)` or sealed item.
  - It never breaks off mid-line into the record, because that would read as a flinch, and a flinch is an invented hesitation.

### 2.4 Budgets

- **Caught devices (D1–D5 and D7):**
  - ≤ 6 per episode (pilot ≤ 7)
  - ≤ 2 per scene
  - never two in one 4-bar phrase (a line and its correction count as one)
  - the same device never twice within 60 s
- **D6 and D8:** ≤ 1 each, on top of those caps.
- **The V.O. sets up; the picture delivers.** Mas never tells a joke in V.O. D8 isn't a joke at all.
- **No device of any kind** inside THE PLAN, a quote card's bar, an exit, a sanctioned sincere (H) beat, the cold open or the button.
- **The candor card.** No caught device in any scene that carries the board's "not consistently candid" card, and the nearest caught line sits at least a scene boundary and 4 bars away. A caught spin line next to the candor charge makes the edit illustrate the charge. *(Ep1: sc 26 carries the card and no V.O.; 26A's D2 starts 4 bars after it, past F1.2.)*
- **Thinning order.** If the table read marks K (corny), Z (drift) or W (read load) near a device, cut in this order:
  1. a D1 whose laugh the picture already gets without the line
  2. D4
  3. D5

  Cut D8 and any D2 that sets up a payoff last. *(Ep1's table read spent rule 1 on sc 26's "the meeting ended early.", and the words moved to the D4 slot.)*

## 3. Mas's inner life

### 3.1 The rule

**Mas never confesses; the audience infers.**
- His face and his V.O. keep the Serene Survivor intact.
- Feelings leak only through the Orb and no-one versions (§1.3), through reflections, and through how much he talks.
- Every look inside him shows image management, and the picture or the Orb catches it. **Intimacy is not endorsement.**

**Scheme small.** He has an intensely strategic inner life about tiny invented stakes: where to stand in the photo, whether to pocket the pin, which suggestion to tap. About the big real events, his surface is blank. That gap is where the comedy lives, and it's also where the guardrails don't bite.

**Everything here is built from the public persona** in [mas-manalt.md](../characters/mas-manalt.md):
- the lunch-order cadence and the lowercase posts
- "I love my current job." [V] and "no equity" [V]
- the badge post [V] and the eulogy post [V]
- poker's "patterns in people" [K]
- the second row
- praise-then-sign (a dramatized pattern)

Nothing comes from private life, with **one exception, and its limits:** the 1993 thread, the ALL-CAPS kid who learned lowercase. It is anchored to the public fact that he got a computer "at eight" [V], and it shows only the screen, the keyboard, the dialog and the juice box: no room and no other person.

### 3.2 What he wants

| Level | Want | Where it shows | Grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What he says** | To be useful, and to love the job | The V.O., never stated as a want | "I love my current job." [V · MAY 16, 2023]; "no equity." |
| **What he shows** | To be liked by every room at once, and to win without being seen to play | The hearts on the beat; praise at noon, the signature by dinner; the calm-off | The praise-then-sign pattern; lowercase as a poker face |
| **What he never says** | **To be asked**: chosen, not merely survived | The second row. CEO of the Year, never Person. The class photo taken without him (Ep7). "gerg never waits to be asked." (Ep1) → Ep12's "to be fired, or at least asked" | The character file's Ep12 want |

### 3.3 What he fears

| Fear | Where it shows | Limits |

### 3.7 Guardrails on the inner life

- **Feelings attach to invented beats, to objects and to his own public lines, never to the real events themselves.** The show's craft (sound, shot size, the drop-out, the `[CU]`) may register the weight of a real event. His tells and his V.O. may not claim a feeling about it. So the firing gets the silence and the close-up, and he carves a mark that night.
- **At a real event his surface stays blank.** No visible calculation: no chain of eyelines before a line, no hover before a choice. He always takes the strip's offer at once (G04).
- **His real acts are never staged as tells.** A real post or a real reply of his never rhymes with a tell taught earlier: no on-beat rhythm and no hover. *(Ep1: the three hearts to Alyi rise at the post's own pace, his face unchanged, one scene after we learned that hearts on the beat are his rhythm game.)*
- **A Mas flashback may rhyme with a real present event, never stage the reason for his choice in it.** Put a rail item or another face between the memory and his real line.
- Never clinical (X3): no anxiety, insomnia, panic, therapy or "breakdown" framing.
- Never family, home or romance (X1, X2). The dark room is a work room, and deliveries come through the rack or the monitor.
- Never a motive at a real event, and never a hesitation on a credibility question.
- **`(REPORTED)`, `(DISPUTED)` and sealed material get the Orb's look or nothing, never a Mas tell.** So there is no glass nudge on `ALYI (REPORTED)` in sc 29, and **tally marks 1 and 2 (the TPOOL ousters) are never touched by him, in any episode.** He carves mark 3 and rests his thumb on it; the Orb may count all three. Ep12's 4× tally replay stays in the show's voice, with the labels on.

- **Their roast comes back in the same episode,** ideally in the same scene (fairness rule 7: no halos).

### 4.3 Rules

1. **Distance tracks interiority.**
   - `[W]` is the world's view.
   - `[M]` and `[2S]` put us in the room with him.
   - `[P]` is conversation.
   - `[PF]`, `[CU]` and `[ECU]` are inside him.

   Tells are only drawn at `[PF]` or closer, so that's the only place they're ever shot.
2. **Open close; earn the wide.** Home rooms never get an establishing wide. A new room gets ≤ 1 bar, or ≤ 2 for a set-piece arrival. Each scene gets one return to `[W]`, for the physical gag or the button.
3. **No exchange longer than two lines stays in `[W]`.**
4. **Two-shot first.** An anchor scene between Mas and a principal establishes the pair in `[2S]` or `[P2]` before the first portrait volley. Attachment comes from sharing a space.
5. **The listener.**
   - Every exchange cuts to the listener on at least one punchline.
   - After a chaos line, hold on Mas, not on the speaker (L4).
   - Every real line lands on a listening face within 1 beat.
   - **Exception: Mas's own real lines** land on a prop consequence or on a face other than the Orb's, never on the Orb's look. The witness looking at him as he says a real line would be the show judging his sincerity (§2.1, §3.7). *(Ep1: the memo lands on the screwdriver taking Alyi's nameplate off.)*
6. **The eyeline rule.** In his POV, every insert is motivated by his look or his touch, and returns to his face or the glass. The rail and the Orb are exempt.
7. **Full-bleed is his; a bezel is the world's.**
   - A screen that fills the frame is seen through his eyes.
   - A screen with its bezel is an object in the world.
   - A cutaway entered through his eyeline stays in his POV: his phone lights, and we push to full-bleed.
   - GLYPH can ride any shot. It is never his.
8. **Screen direction.** Mas stays in the left third, in three-quarter view, at every size, with the other character on the right. The one exception is the lens look.
9. **Frame people at the distance he keeps them.** *(See also §3.8: share the frame.)*
   - In his POV, a close-up is something he gives. The board stays a corner thumbnail in pass two, while Gerg gets a tile big enough to act in.
   - **Fairness exception:** anyone who speaks in a room with him gets the right-hand window, and exits give everyone full closeness.
10. **The smile stays one pixel at every scale.** In `[CU]` it's even smaller relative to the face, and that's the joke. He doesn't blink (§3.6).
11. **Cut, don't zoom, and cut on the grid.** Volleys cut on beats, holds are counted in beats, and set-pieces run in 4-bar phrases (L7).
12. **Pay for close coverage with the wide.** Each added close cut removes one drawn event from a wide: a walk step, a background pose, a redundant object hop. The visual-events cap (≈ 1,300) does not go up.
13. **Dark rooms make close shots cheap.** Only the lit area carries detail. A desk edge, the cyan cone and the rack LEDs are a whole medium plate.
14. **Medium rigs and quiet beats are for principals.** Cameos stay in `[W]` and `[P]`, with plates.

## 5. V.O. style guide

### 5.1 The voice

- **The performer** is the same as on camera. Use a synthetic stock preset until the part is cast, and never a clone.
- **The sound:** close-miked, dry, with no room tone or reverb. It's a touch slower than in scenes: 110–125 wpm against his 125–135. It's a story he has told many times, told to one person.
- **The mix:** it sits at dialogue level. The score thins to one instrument, or drops out, under it. Never put a sting or a swell under a line.
- **Timing:** it starts on a beat, and ends at least 1 beat before a cut, a spoken line or a must-read rail item.

### 5.2 On screen

- **Position:** one line, left-aligned, directly above the band (x 12, baseline y 198). Use a 1-px `N0` shadow.
- **No frame:** no box, portrait, name plate or quotation marks. His account sits on top of the record.
- **Colour:** his cyan, one ramp step down.
- **Legibility:** frame every shot that carries a V.O. line so its text band (y 182–203) sits on N-ramp shadow (hoodie, desk edge, dark room), never on the cyan key or a cyan-lit face. Test it for W (read load) at the slate.
- **Speed:** it types at his rate, the slowest in the show (0.5 characters per frame), about one line per bar. The voice is the clock.
- **What it never shares the screen with:** it never goes in the rail, never over the dialogue box, and never at the same moment as a toast or a rail item. Stagger them by at least 1 beat, so there is one must-read at a time.

### 5.3 Grammar and length

- **Lowercase always**, including "i" and names ("gerg", "the board"). **The V.O. never slips into capitals.** The slips belong to his posts and his speech (Ep7, Ep10). The V.O. is his most controlled voice.
- **Tense:** past tense. Use the present tense only for self-descriptions ("i don't keep score.").
- **Where it's told from (PROPOSED, showrunner):** the fact freeze, Ep9's `TODAY` card, not an unnamed point after the finale. Past tense through Ep9; **present tense from Ep10**, when he has caught up with his own story and is no longer ahead of us on anything. That doesn't pre-decide whether he survives Eps 10–12 with his voice intact, it keeps his calm past tense from narrating `????` events as settled, and it matches the V.O. thinning to 3–5 lines a week in Eps 10–12. *Default until ruled: past tense through Ep9; Eps 10–12 drafts avoid V.O. that implies an ending.*
- **Punctuation:**
  - full stops only
  - a semicolon is his 1-beat pause
  - no exclamation marks, question marks, dashes or ellipses
- **Length:**
  - 8 words or fewer as a rule, 10 at most, and ≤ 45 glyphs
  - one sentence, or two only if the second is 3 words or fewer
- **Vocabulary:**
  - plain words: no jargon, no hype words and no insider references, so every line lands for THE OUTSIDER
  - "i think", "super" and "extremely": at most once each per episode across all the V.O.
- **Tone:** generous about everyone. Never angry, never sarcastic, never ironic about himself; the irony belongs to the picture.
- **Feelings:** it may deny a feeling. It never claims one.
- **Address:** never "you." The closeness of the mic does the confiding, and the glance does the addressing.
- **Variety:** no two V.O. lines in an episode open with the same two words.

### 5.4 Volume and placement

- **5–8 lines per episode** (pilot ≤ 8), ≤ 60 words and ≤ 30 s in total.
- **How it counts:** V.O. words don't count toward his ≤ 20% share of scene words. **Laughs are logged in two kinds (PROPOSED for the pacing owner):** laughs *by* Mas (his spoken lines and his rimshots) stay capped at 20–35% of the episode's laughs; laughs *at* Mas (a caught V.O. line, whose laugh the picture delivers) are logged apart and are already limited by the device caps. Without the split, a narrating lead is AMBER every week. Until it's ruled, log both and report both.
- **Spread it through the episode:**
  - V.O. appears in at least 3 of the 4 movements
  - ≤ 3 lines per scene, and ≤ 2 back to back
  - he narrates more when he's cornered, so one movement may carry up to half the episode's lines; a sanctioned long movement (the pilot's 7:13 Blip) may carry ≤ 5
  - at least one line before the episode's cornered movement, so the voice is heard "throughout" (Ep1: sc 6 and sc 18, plus an optional sc 10 line)
- **The first line** comes only after he's been established in a room, at least 45 s into Act One.
- **The season curve:** Eps 1–3 establish the voice (7–8 lines), Eps 4–9 settle (5–7), and Eps 10–12 thin out (3–5) as the machine's POV grows. The narrator's share runs opposite to the dread curve.

### 5.5 Where V.O. is forbidden

- within 1 bar of any real line, dated quote card, name card or freeze, or any `(REPORTED)`, `(DISPUTED)` or sealed item (the silence, §2.3)
- inside the drop-out (D6): the silence is the laugh
- as a caught line in any scene that carries the candor card, or within a scene boundary and 4 bars of it (§2.4)
- at contested or credibility moments:
  - the reasons for the firing
  - the Ep2 NDA line
  - Ep7's HUG and the Pentagon deal
  - the Ep8 courtroom and "yes."
  - the sealed memo
  - the New Yorker piece
- in government proceedings: the Senate, the UN and the Security Council
- during THE PLAN, GLYPH or TERMINAL, and inside any exit
- over another character's punchline or sincere beat
- in sanctioned sincere beats, including **F9.3, the one memory he doesn't narrate**
- in the cold open, the intro and the button (the button has ≤ 3 spoken words, on camera)
- in the 1993 tier, until Ep12

### 5.8 Lint

- At a real-event beat, a V.O. line containing *knew, planned, decided, wanted, meant, realized, because, so that, on purpose* or *strategy* is an automatic guardrail flag (G). So is *candid, honest, quiet* or *sincere*: they sit one step from the board's charge or claim a feeling.
- A V.O. claim about his conduct over a span of time ("that weekend", "i never") is checked against everything the episode has shown in that span, not just the scene it's in.
- A K mark on any V.O. line counts as K on Mas, which is RED.

- or his `[PF]`
