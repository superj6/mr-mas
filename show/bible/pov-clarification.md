# POV clarification (showrunner, 2026-09-25): binding for pov-and-framing.md and all rewrites

**This is not first-person camera.** The show uses **limited third person** centred on MAS:

**The camera is free.** We see Mas from outside, and other characters get full scenes and close-ups.

**The storytelling is filtered through Mas:**
- He narrates, in sparing lowercase V.O.
- Scenes are framed by what he notices, how he reads people and what he cares about.
- We mostly know what he knows.
- His account can be self-serving or edited: rewinds, relabels, "that's not how it went." The show lets the audience catch it.

**Leaving his POV is the exception, and it is signposted.** Use a card (e.g. `WHAT THEY DIDN'T KNOW`), the other side's pass in a told-twice structure, or a cold open in another timeline.

**Reference shows, and what to borrow:**

| Show | What to borrow |
|---|---|
| Mr. Robot (closest) | Confiding, quietly unreliable narrator in a tech world; reveals that reframe what we saw |
| The Wolf of Wall Street / The Big Short | Rise-to-power narration that rewinds and corrects itself; explainer asides (our THE PLAN) |
| House of Cards | Asides to camera as complicity; for Mas, a glance to the lens rather than a speech |
| Fleabag | Camera as confidant; what she hides from us matters |
| BoJack Horseman, Barry, Better Call Saul | Anchored on one flawed lead, with occasional scenes or episodes from others' side; Saul's cold opens in other timelines |
| Death Note, Kaguya-sama | Inner monologue: calm exterior, scheming or anxious interior, played for comedy and tension |
| Dexter | Composed-mask V.O. against the reality |
| I, Tonya, Rashomon | Competing accounts of the same events; "The Blip, told twice" |
