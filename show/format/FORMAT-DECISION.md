## 1. Recommendation

1. **Twelve episodes at 22:00 each. No change to the episode count.** Story time is 20:45. Runtime band 21:30–22:30 for every episode except Ep9. Ep3 and Ep12 may come in as low as **20:30** rather than pad.
2. **Ep9 is the season's one extended episode: up to 26:00, in five movements.** It is the "TODAY" hinge where the real record catches up to the air date and the rail simply keeps rolling into Ep10, with no speculation card (showrunner, 2026-09-25). The 26:00 is a ceiling, not a target. Ep9 goes through Ep1-style A/B/C triage first. If triage lands it at 22:30 or less, it ships at 22. Any extra time goes to a fifth movement of *played scenes* (the Security Council, the state dinner), never to longer news runs.
3. **Every 22 is built to split.** The midpoint act-out falls in the 10:00–14:15 window, so any episode can ship as two 11s if distribution asks. The writing costs nothing extra. The release costs about 4–5 agent-h per episode (a new 30–45 s B-half cold open and a 15 s intro cut), paid only if that option is used.
4. **The proof comes first:** *THE BLIP, TOLD TWICE* (about 7:00, Ep1's Act 3), built from the three calibration pieces. It measures the unmeasured dialogue rate and the first real laugh rates. Its shots go straight back into Ep1.
5. **Short formats are extras only.** Besides the proof, THE PLAN blueprints (45 s each, accurate and self-contained) are the free social cut-downs. No other companion shorts are scheduled for season 1.
6. **Eps 1–9 invent scenes, not props. Eps 10–12 invent meters, not scenes.** The first nine have more real events than any format holds but too few played scenes. The last three are all scenes and need visible progression (skyline states, the countdown clock, question-mark date chyrons).
7. **Fact freeze at Ep9's lock (planned about Oct 30).** Ep9's `TODAY` card carries the freeze date. Eps 10–12 are re-outlined against what really happened after Sep 24 before they are scripted (§7.7).

## 2. Rationale

### 2.1 Is there enough entertaining content, and for what length?

Story time needed at 22:00 is 20:45. Measured against content-density's grades (A strong, B works, C accurate but not funny):

| Ep | As drafted | A + B material | Verdict at 22 | What closes the gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ≈ 27:30 | ≈ 24:20 | Overstuffed | Cut ≈ 6:45 (Act 2 and the cards) → 20:48 with the Blip intact |
| 2 | ≈ 20:45 | ≈ 16:55 | Fits, with 2:45 of C-grade news | Trim the C beats, spend ≈ 2:00 on played scenes |
| 3 | ≈ 16:45 | ≈ 14:25 | Thin | ≈ 4:00–4:30 of invented scenes, led by THE LINE AT THE PODIUM |
| 4 | ≈ 20:50 | ≈ 18:15 | Fits | Trim the cold open and Paris; ≈ 1:25 spare |
| 5–8 | 18–25 (outline level) | — | Fit to slightly dense | Ordinary triage |
| 9 | 23–27 | — | Overstuffed | Triage, then up to 26:00 (§1.2) |
| 10–12 | 14–22 | — | Fits to thin | Played scenes breathe; add meters and THE PLAN to Ep12 |

**The answer:** the A+B material in Eps 1–4 averages about **18:30** against the 20:45 needed. Filling that last two minutes is *not padding*: what is missing in Eps 2–3 is exactly the invented in-the-moment scenes the balance asks for. A 22 therefore forces the right kind of invention. A shorter runtime would fit the drafts as they stand and lock in the imbalance: Eps 2–3 would stay at 38–40% I.

### 2.2 Only 22 holds both halves of the balance

- **In the moment:** four anchor scenes of 1:30–3:00 (Mas's calm against everyone else's chaos) need about 8–10 minutes.
- **Macro:** a spine of 8–12 dated events, plus the fairness floor (three camps, Misanthropic, both parties: about 30–40 s even at its most compressed), plus THE PLAN, needs another 5–6 minutes.
- **Running gags:** the gag architecture (about 70 gags, five-part threads) needs room to set up and pay off.
- **Only a 22 fits all three.** Every 11-minute cut in content-density either drops macro to 2–9% or shrinks scenes to under a minute. At 6–8 minutes the show becomes a sketch.

### 2.3 What the intro tells us about production time

| Measure | Intro (30 s) | One 22-minute episode (mid) |
|---|---|---|
| All-in agent time | 13.8 agent-h, about **28 agent-h per minute** | **118 agent-h** (74–179), about **5.5 agent-h per new minute**, a fifth of the intro's per-second cost |
| Wall-clock at about 8 agents | 2 h 20 min | about **15 h** (8–30), about 1.25 machine-days at 12 h/day |
| What drives cost | about 4 visual events per second | about 1 event per second; picture ≈ 0.4 agent-min × seconds + 3.5 agent-min × events |

- **Cost follows spectacle, not runtime.** Set-pieces are 17% of the minutes and 44% of the picture cost. Portrait dialogue in existing rooms is the cheapest footage the show has (about 1.3 agent-h per minute).
- **The balance and the budget point the same way.** The cheapest way to make an episode (played scenes in home rooms, macro riding on state changes of existing assets) is also the fix the drafts need. Nothing in this format trades entertainment for cost.
- **22 is the cheapest length per minute** (5.5 agent-h, against 6.7 at 11 and 7.4 at 7), because set-pieces stay per episode and shorter formats cut the cheap dialogue first.

### 2.4 The limits that actually bind

1. **Showrunner review, not machine time.** About 100–120 h across the season at the current per-chunk review rate. The streamlined protocol in §7.6 brings it to about 60 h.
2. **The dialogue rate is inferred, not measured.** The swing is 10–26 agent-h per episode. The calibration trio settles it in 3–5 wall-hours (§7.4, gate G1).
3. **One agent holds about 10 s of set-piece or 30–60 s of dialogue** before its context fills. That makes about 65 picture tasks per episode, and it is why set-pieces are written in 10 s phrases.
4. **The live edge.** Real events keep happening during production. Ep10's anchors (the Sep 29 meeting, the Oct 1 deadline) will be history before Ep10 is built (§7.7).

## 4. The episode template (22:00)

| **Total** | | **22:00** | | | **36** | **≈ 84 agent-h picture** (80–85; ≈ 118 all-in) |

A movement is the natural planning unit: about 20 agent-h of picture, or about **2.5 wall-hours at 8 agents**.

### 4.2 Inside a movement

Each movement braids the two halves of the balance ([pacing-model §3.2](pacing-model.md#32-inside-a-movement-the-braid)):

```
ROOM ─────────── CLOCK ────────── SPECTACLE ──────────── ROOM ──────── ACT-OUT
anchor scene     ≤ 0:45 run,      0:40–1:30 set-piece,   ≥ 0:30        0:10–0:30
1:30–3:00        or a delivery    or THE PLAN → set-piece a smaller beat a turn (T)
(in the moment)  (macro)          (both at once)
```

- A movement opens in a room, or on a delivery into one. It never opens on a chyron run.
- No two macro blocks touch, and no two big set-pieces touch: at least 30 s of played scene sits between them.
- **Macro arrives as a prop where it can** (the delivery ladder, [pacing-model §8.1](pacing-model.md#81-the-delivery-ladder)): the lawsuit through the skylight beats a chyron. News that must be a run plays on the dark-room monitor with Mas and THE ORB reacting, which turns macro time into played time.
- **At least one meter visibly moves per movement** (the tally, the collars, the podium, the class photo, the skyline, the countdown: [pacing-model §11.3](pacing-model.md#113-progression-meters)). That is progression at zero runtime and near-zero cost.

### 4.3 The balance and the minute contract

| Measure | Target (22) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I · in the moment | 45–55% (9:20–11:25), of which anchor scenes ≥ 8:00 | The room is the show |
| S · spectacle | 15–20% (3:07–4:09); 4–6 set-pieces (S2+), each a spectacle + laugh + progression triple | Tech as visual spectacle |
| M · macro | 20–25% (4:09–5:11) | The real-events spine |
| F · flashback | 5–8% (1:02–1:40), per the [flashback map](../timeline/flashback-map.md); Ep1 ≤ 20 s | Backstory where it motivates the present |
| P · THE PLAN | 45 s (18 bars), once | The one full stop |
| Jokes | 4–6 per story minute (about 85–120 per episode); a big laugh at least every 2:00; no 20 s dead air | Entertainment floor |
| Progression | A real event or arc step at least every 2:00; the spine changes state at least 3 times | Macro floor |
| Mas | ≤ 20% of his scenes' words, 20–35% of the laughs (mostly reactions) | The deadpan engine |
| Audience | ≥ 60% of laughs land for both the AI community and a general audience; ≤ 20% insider-only (the rest go to eggs and fine print) | Both audiences |

## 5. Teleplay page target

### 5.1 The target

**36 pages for a 22-minute episode, on the house page, with a band of 33–40.** Above 40 the draft is overlong; below 33 it is underwritten. Either way, **the slate animatic decides**: 20:45 of story, ±0:30.

For Ep9 at 26:00: 43 pages (band 40–47).

### 5.2 Why not "one page ≈ 45–60 s" across the board

That rate is right for **dialogue pages**, and it would put a 22 at 21–28 pages. This show is only about half dialogue. Its set-pieces, chyron runs, cards and THE PLAN are written beat by beat and play much faster per page. The blended rate at the template's centre:

| Mode | Share | Story time | Seconds per page | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I · dialogue scenes | 50% | 10:23 | ≈ 50 (45–60; Mas's holds push it long, the chaos volleys push it short) | 12.5 |
| S · set-pieces | 17% | 3:32 | ≈ 30 (one action paragraph per 4-bar phrase) | 7.1 |
| M · runs and deliveries | 22% | 4:34 | ≈ 25 (one item per 1–2 bars, each item its own paragraph) | 11.0 |
| F · flashbacks | 7% | 1:27 | ≈ 30 | 2.9 |
| P · THE PLAN | 4% | 0:45 | ≈ 30 (four labelled beats with bar counts) | 1.5 |
| Headers, transitions, act-outs | — | — | — | ≈ 1 |
| **Total** | | **20:45** | **≈ 35 blended** | **≈ 36** |

pacing-model's approximate 38–46 ([§3.9](pacing-model.md#39-the-script-page-budget-approximate)) assumed looser action writing. With the house page below, 36 is the number. A 24-page draft of this show would time out at about 14 minutes of story.

### 5.3 The house page

Scripts in this room are Markdown, so "a page" needs a definition an agent can count:

- **One page = 55 lines** in screenplay layout: action wraps at 61 characters, dialogue at 35, parentheticals at 25. Sluglines, character cues and transitions take one line each, with one blank line between elements.
- That comes to about **7,000–7,500 words** for 36 pages.
- **Notation to carry in the script** (it costs no page lines when inline):
  - durations in bars and beats on every timed non-dialogue element (`[4 BARS]`, `[HOLD 2 BEATS]`)
  - the style tags from the [intro master script](../intro/SCRIPT.md) (`[BASE]`, `[1-BIT]`, `[EARLY-WEB16]`, `[2-TONE FREEZE]`, `[GLYPH-MASKED]`, `[LEDGER]`)
  - the mode letter per beat (I / S / M / F / P), so the slate animatic and critics can compute shares
  - the fact tags on every real line, per [guardrails §4](../bible/guardrails.md#4-how-facts-appear-on-screen), and `[INVENTED]` on invented lines
  - `CARD:` for a name-card freeze and `PLATE:` for a no-freeze plate
- **Suggested file:** `episodes/epNN/script.md`. If the room has already chosen a layout, keep it and count pages with the line model above.

### 7.6 Review protocol (proposed)

At about 65 chunk reviews of 4 minutes each, plus four gates, review costs 8–10 h per episode and becomes the calendar. Proposed instead:

- **Critic agents review the chunks:** THE EDITOR checks timing and grid; a pixel QA agent checks palette, whole-pixel motion and faces. Chunks go to the showrunner only if flagged.
- **The showrunner watches one scene reel per scene,** at 1×, with a notes pass. That is about 14 scenes, or about 1.5 h per episode.
- **The four gates stay with the showrunner:** script read (slate animatic), animatic, picture lock, mix.
- **Result:** about 4.5 h per episode, about 60 h for the season.

### 7.7 The live edge: fact freeze

- **The freeze is Ep9's lock**, planned for about Oct 30. Ep9's `TODAY` card reads the freeze date, not Sep 24.
- **Real events between Sep 25 and the freeze** go into Ep9's tag if they're small, or into Ep10 as its real anchors (Ep10 already plans to re-verify the Sep 29 meeting, DevDay and the Oct 1 deadline the week they happen).
- **Speculative chyrons stay ahead of the release date.** Never air `OCT 2026?` about a month that has already happened. At the freeze, Eps 10–12's question-mark windows move forward as needed.
- **If release falls well after season lock**, run one re-verify pass on Eps 9–10 before release.
- **Eps 1–8 are built first** because their record is fixed. Ep9's body (Jul → Sep 24) can go into picture before the freeze; only its tag waits.
