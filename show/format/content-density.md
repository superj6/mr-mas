**Season in one paragraph.** Make **22 minutes** the format. It's the only length where both halves of the showrunner's balance fit: 3–4 in-the-moment scenes at 1.5–3 minutes each, plus a real-event spine of 8–12 dated beats. The fairness rules alone put a floor under the macro side: at least three camps, Misanthropic, and both parties in every episode. At 11 minutes the cuts below leave only 2–9% macro time, so the show turns into set-pieces without the "you can't make this up" spine. At 6–8 minutes it's a sketch. Eps 1–9 are rich in macro and short on interaction, so they need invented scenes more than invented events. Eps 10–12 are the reverse. Ep1 and Ep9 are overstuffed, and Ep3 and Ep12 are thin. Use 11 minutes only as a split of a 22 (every 22 built with a clean midpoint), and 6–8 minutes only as companion shorts. The first short should be THE BLIP, which also makes the best production test. Full reasoning is in [§8](#8-season-level-recommendation).

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Story-time budgets
| Format | Runtime | Fixed overhead | Story time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 min | 22:00 | Intro 0:30 (locked), filename/disclaimer card 0:02, end credits ≈0:43 (the legal card alone needs ≈20 s of read time) | **≈20:45** |
| 11 min | 11:00 | Cut-down intro 0:15 (not yet specced), card 0:02, credits ≈0:28 | **≈10:15** |
| 6–8 min | ≈7:00 | Title sting 0:05, card 0:02, credits ≈0:23 | **≈6:30** |

Name-card freezes happen inside story time, so they're counted there.

### 2.2 Beat classes (the brief's rule of thumb)
| Class | Floor → best | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scene** | 60 → 180 s | Two or more characters in one place, played in real time, escalating | The Email Séance, the Senate, GATESTAR DIALS OUT |
| **Set-piece** | 30 → 150 s | Tech as visual spectacle | The odometer drill, the pebble, SHIPMAS |
| **News/montage** | 10 → 30 s per item | A dated event, a chyron and one visual gag | The TIDDER troll, the hydra head |
| **Gag** | 10 → 45 s | A character beat under half a minute | "noted.", SYDNEY's egg timer |
| **Flashback** | As specced, plus transitions | — | F3.1 (40 s) |
| **THE PLAN** | 45 s | — | — |
| **Button** | 10 → 45 s | — | The podium turns |

### 2.3 Three numbers per episode
- **Floor:** every beat at its minimum readable length. This is what the beat sheet costs if nothing gets to breathe.
- **As drafted:** scenes, set-pieces, flashbacks, THE PLAN and buttons at their best length. News and gags stay at floor, because stretching them is padding. Name cards are added on top.
- **Genuinely entertaining:** the as-drafted time of the beats graded **A** (a strong comic engine, or a real line that's a gift) or **B** (works and does its job). **C** beats are informational: accurate and on theme, but not funny at any length. They're the first cut candidates, or they become background eggs.

### 2.4 The balance metric
Every beat gets one mode:
- **I, in-the-moment:** a character plays a moment in real time (a conversation, a reaction, a confrontation).
- **M, macro:** compressed time. Date chyrons, news, money figures, headlines.
- **S, spectacle:** a set-piece that carries macro information as visual comedy, so it does both jobs at once.
- **F** is a flashback and **P** is THE PLAN.

A working target for a 22 is about **45–55% I, 20–25% M, 15–20% S, 5–8% F and 4% P.**

### 2.5 Hidden overheads
- **Name cards cost 2–3 s each.** The window opens in three held steps, the tagline types at 2 characters per frame, and the stamp lands at frame 14. Read time follows [shot-table §6](../intro/shot-table.md#6-text-registry-and-read-time-lint): 0.25 s + 0.05 s per glyph. Ep1's 33 cards come to **1:05–1:40 of frozen screen.** Every cut below caps cards at about 16.
- **Real quotes are fixed length** under the source-fidelity rule. Long ones (the GOP platform line, the employee letter, the extinction statement) should be read as cards, not spoken.
- **Synthetic stock voices** read flatter and slower than performers, and deadpan real lines live on timing. Budget about +10% on dialogue scenes until human actors are cast.
- **Running gags cost little runtime if they're embedded:** the tracker's rule is 5 s or less per appearance. They do cost *attention*, though. Ep1 sets up 27 of them.

### Where it's thin
Nowhere in total. Act 1 is light on interaction (28% I): the launch is told as a set-piece, not a scene. The Blip (Act 3) is the pilot's strongest material, and at 7–9 minutes it's already sized as a proper second half.

### Balance
The as-drafted split (52% I) looks healthy, but it's lopsided. Nearly all the interaction sits in Act 3, and Act 2 is a news reel with two scenes inside it. The 22 cut below evens it out: 55% I, 22% M, 19% S.

## 8. Season-level recommendation

1. **Make 22 minutes the format, twelve episodes, about 20:45 of story each.**
   - At 22 minutes both halves of the balance fit: 3–4 scenes of 1.5–3 minutes, plus 8–12 dated beats.
   - The gag architecture needs this length too: about 70 gags, five-part threads, and the setup, escalation and payoff matrix.
   - So do the fairness floor (three camps, Misanthropic and both parties per episode: about 30–40 s even at its most compressed) and THE PLAN.
   - Every 11-minute cut above either drops macro to 2–9% or shrinks scenes below a minute.

2. **Cut the pilot to 22; don't extend it.** A 28-minute pilot with 33 cards is the biggest risk to keeping viewers. The cuts in [§3](#22-minutes-cut-or-merge--2048-with-16-cards) get Ep1 to 20:48 with the Blip intact. If the platform wants a two-part premiere, split it at the `HE WILL BE FIRED TOMORROW` catch-up.

3. **Give Ep3 about four minutes of invented scenes**, led by X3.3 THE LINE AT THE PODIUM. If distribution allows variable runtimes, Ep3 can run 19 minutes with 2–3 minutes of invention. Don't pad it with news.

4. **Use one episode shape** (22 minutes, about 20:45 of story):

| Block | Time | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0:45–1:15 | ≈5% | One location, one joke, ends on a hook. The intro no longer previews the episode |
| 3–4 anchor scenes (I) | 8:00–10:00 | 40–50% | Two or more characters in one room, with a real event as the spine |
| 2–3 macro blocks (M) | 3:00–4:00 | 15–20% | 45–90 s each, date chyrons, routed through reusable screens (item 6) |
| 1–2 set-pieces (S) | 2:00–3:00 | 10–15% | Tech as spectacle |
| Flashbacks (F) | 1:00–1:40 | 5–8% | As mapped |
| THE PLAN (P) | 0:45 | 4% | — |
| Button | 0:15–0:30 | — | — |
| First-appearance name cards | ≤16, ≈0:40 | — | Card only people who speak a real line or recur in 2+ later episodes |

5. **Build recurring invented scene engines, not more one-off props.** These are cheap rooms built once, where the connective tissue can be *played* instead of stated:
   - **Mas's dark room plus THE ORB** (from Ep1 Act 2): a 20–40 s debrief on the monitor once or twice an episode.
   - **The NopeAI lobby:** the `DAYS SINCE` sign and the revolving door.
   - **THE PODIUM hill / the GOLD OVAL line:** supplicants.
   - **Misanthropic's lighthouse:** MARIO drafts, ADELINA translates "in plain English."
   - **zAI's rafters:** Nole heckles from above.
   - **The boardroom.**

6. **Route macro through screens and the UI band.** This is where the pixel adventure structure pays for itself:
   - **News:** a montage block plays on the dark-room monitor while Mas and the Orb react, so an M block becomes partly I at almost no cost (the room exists).
   - **Running-gag state in the inventory band:** the tally, the collar count, gold threads, the Orb's verdict, the badge. It reads as progression in every scene with zero runtime.
   - **One point-and-click scene per episode where it's earned**, like X2.2. Keep it on the beat, per the brief's rule.

7. **Fix Ep9 and Ep12 at the outline stage.**
   - Ep9 needs Ep1-style triage: sort its 37 beats into A/B/C, route the September news through the dark-room monitor, and cap cards. Alternatively, make it the season's one extended episode (about 26 minutes, the "TODAY" midseason finale).
   - Ep12 needs its dinner scenes to breathe (the seating chart, the kill-switch standoff, the vote to fire Mas) and a THE PLAN beat. Its endings montage is already correctly short (about 4 s per item), so don't grow it.

8. **Use short formats as companions, not the primary format.**
   - **11 minutes:** write every 22 with a clean act break near 10:30 (Ep1 at the chyron, Ep2 at THE PLAN, Ep3 at the $157B, Ep4 at high noon), so it can be released as two halves if distribution demands it.
   - **6–8 minutes:** one set-piece per short, with 10 s fairness beats added.
   - **The first short should be THE BLIP, told twice (≈6:30).**
     - About 80% of it is in-the-moment dialogue, the kind of portrait scene REPORT.md rates "nearly free."
     - It needs about five rooms: the Vegas suite, the video grid, the HQ lobby, the MACROSOFT room, the boardroom.
     - It exercises the GLYPH tile dissolve.
     - It reuses Ep1's assets, and it's the pilot's strongest material. That makes it a proof of concept and a production-time benchmark in one.

## 9. Inputs for the production-time estimate

Density drives cost through **rooms and new characters, not minutes.** REPORT.md's pixel rates: a new room is 1–2 days; a new character is about 1 day for the room sprite plus 1–2 days for the portrait; dialogue scenes are nearly free; walk-ins, slapstick and crowds need extra drawings. The counts below are taken from the beats, ±3.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3 | ≈14 → ≈13 (the inventions reuse hub rooms) | 19 → 14 | 6 | 95 s: EARLY-WEB16 + LEDGER (F3.2); BASE for F3.1 and F3.3 | **The stadium (HOW MANY R'S?)** |

- **The cheapest density lever is room consolidation.** The 22 cuts above remove 25–35% of Ep1–2's one-off rooms, mostly by moving news onto screens. That also raises the in-the-moment share.
- **Add a cameo tier.** A one-beat character gets a portrait only, with no room sprite. That's about 1–2 days instead of 2–3.
- **Stage crowds as held, tiled drawings and put the acting in the portraits.** For HOW MANY R'S?, that's MAON's booth, the owner's box and Mario's rival box. It suits the style and the balance at once.
