# Interior voice: how the reference works get inside a lead (research, 2026-09-27)

> **Showrunner, 2026-09-27:** "don't make mas's unsound narration forced/corny. it is hard to tell attach to mas as there is little that tells about his thoughts throughout, everything still feels distance"

**What this is:** a study of 16 works, for the rules in [mas-inner-voice](../../bible/mas-inner-voice.md). Script counts come from the published draft screenplays (sources below), so aired cuts will differ. Tags: **M** measured, **E** estimate.

## What makes narration fail

- **Describing the picture.** McKee's test: strip the V.O. and the story should still play.
- **Stating feelings.** *Adaptation* parodies exactly this.
- **Explaining the scheme.** This is the Death Note complaint.
- **Wall-to-wall use.** The Kaguya narrator is "spice", not sauce.
- **Quotable aphorisms.** Frank's lines became a supercut of "slickest lines".
- **Talking at the worst moment.** The good shows go quiet there.

## Recommendations for Mas (adopted in the bible guide)

- **Addressee:** himself, with the 1993 kid as a silent addressee (his running answer to `HOW DO I WIN?`). Not the viewer, who is one more audience to perform for. Not the Orb, which is the witness that catches him.
- **Timing:** in the moment. A retrospective narrator either leaks the Act Four firing or has to hide it.
- **Density:** 15–20 lines, about 200–250 words for 22 minutes (about 8% of runtime), in 3–4 clusters from Act One. No lines from the call through the firing. In clustered scenes his inner words at least match his spoken words.
- **Rules:**
  1. Read others, not himself.
  2. Stay present, in the room.
  3. Predict, and let the picture pay it off.
  4. Put "super." and the thought behind it in the same beat, and never explain it.
  5. Get the humour from precision.
  6. Let rhythm carry the feeling.
  7. Go silent at the big moments.
  8. No jargon.
  9. Be reliable about others and unreliable about himself.
  10. Keep the lines mostly true: a quarter or fewer caught.
  11. Let the effort show once an episode.
  12. Cluster, don't sprinkle.
- **Where the bible clashed:** pov-and-framing §5's past tense, its 60-word cap and its caught-line default. The rest (lowercase, never claiming a feeling, never "you", silence at real events) fits.
