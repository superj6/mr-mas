## The short version

The season's spine is right:
- The three movements end on real cliffs: the podium turns (Ep3), the speculation card (Ep9), and the switch left on plus the dinner invite (Ep11).
- The long threads each escalate by one reveal per appearance and pay off in the finale: Cancel and 1993, the tally, the Orb's verdicts, the capital letters and the Intern's desk.
- RUMPT's arc is the cleanest in the show.

The reel also shows four problems:
- **The POV slips.** Around Ep5 the show stops being told through Mas, and it never fully comes back.
- **The narration is flat.** Mas's voice-over is the same across all twelve episodes.
- **The endgame repeats itself.** Eps 9–12 replay the same beats.
- **The finale misses one payoff.** It pays off every gag except the one thing Mas wants.

Every fix below works inside episodes. No merge or reorder is needed.

## What's working (keep it)

1. **Cancel is the season's spine, and it's bookended.**
   - 1.03: in 1993, Cancel is greyed out.
   - 1.29: the board clicks it, and it works.
   - 1.42: it greys out again.
   - 12.37: `HOW DO I WIN?`, with Cancel greyed out.
   - 12.39: the screen answers `define "win."`

   One idea is planted in the pilot's cold open and answered in the finale's last minute.
2. **The machine's rise is the best-built through-line.** Every step is one promotion; it never jumps.
   - Ep6: `RESERVED: SEP 2026` (6.22), whose lamp lights at 6.44.
   - Ep9: the goal is "met" (9.21).
   - Ep10: czar on a test it graded itself; 360 reviewers who are all itself; the dealer Mas can't read; RESEARCHER a year early.
   - Ep11: it trains its successor and reads the clause literally.
   - Ep12: it hosts the dinner and vetoes the firing.
3. **The thesis lands as a joke, twice:** "it thinks i'm part of it." (11.36) and "people prefer a human face. you're the best at it." (12.24). Both turn the distillation gag (4.20 → 7.27 → 8.16) on Mas himself.
4. **RUMPT's arc is the clearest in the show.**
   - Ep2: hands only, and SO SCARY.
   - 3.41: the podium turns on a coin slot.
   - The label gun renames things: GULF, GENIUS, WAR, SUPER.
   - 8.08: "Who?"
   - 12.10: `PRESIDENT → USER`.

   Prop parity with NEDIB holds.
5. **The reserved tells are spent where the bible put them:** the bead (7.12), the capital I (7.13), "Huh." (10.13) and the ring (12.40). The Orb goes from `probably` (6.36) to `SIDE: UNCLEAR` (12.41). The tagline goes from *near* (4.01), to *now* cut off at the dash (9.01), to `ours.` (12.05).
6. **Dread builds as the pacing model asks.** The hooks thicken through Eps 6–8 (the RESERVED lamp, the vault that waves, the log nobody reads), then the endgame speeds up into cliffs.
7. **The flashback threads escalate one reveal at a time, and nothing is front-loaded.**
   - THE WOODROSE ×5: 3.17, 5.26, 6.27, 10.18, 12.11–12.13.
   - 1993 ×4: 1.03, 4.18, 7.14, 12.36.
   - Nole's grievance, told out of order and paid off on the stand: 2.08 → 6.26–6.28 → 8.18–8.25.
8. **The set-pieces are inventive, and each one carries a mechanism:**
   - the Money-Go-Round
   - THE HUG's third arm
   - the calendar's verdict
   - the Rashomon renders
   - the politeness loop
   - the 360 review
   - the bubble-sorted seating chart
9. **Misanthropic is roasted in all twelve episodes.** The cross-cutting politicians (MOSWEN's veto, YELWAH, SITNASED, NONNAB) do the fairness work they were cast for.

## Top 10 structural notes

Ranked by how much of the season each one touches.

### 1. From Ep5, the show stops being told through Mas (Eps 5, 7, 9, 11, 12)

**Evidence:** Mas share falls from 78% (Ep1) to 38%, 36%, 43%, 26% and 49%. Only Eps 1 and 2 meet the 70% floor.
- The lost time is macro and ensemble beats that neither include him nor are signposted as exits: Kram's draft night (5.12–5.14), the Pentagon standoff (7.29–7.32), Ep9's news run (9.26–9.32) and the Researcher's office (11.01–11.15).
- In the Ep11 sheet, Mas appears in 9 of 44 stills.

Read this way, the season plays as an omniscient news satire, not as a story told through Mas.

**Fix:**
- **Anchor scenes:** put Mas in every one.
- **Macro:** play it on his screens (the dark-room monitor, his phone, the rail), with his glass or his hand in frame.
- **Exits:** anything that has to play without him gets a signposted exit card and counts toward the cap of 12% and 3 exits. `MEANWHILE, IN THE SANDBOX.` (9.02) is the model to copy.
- **Ep7:** play 7.29–7.32 as a feed on his dark-room monitor with the glass in the foreground, so the hug (7.33) is the moment he walks into a story he's been watching.
- **Ep11 and Ep5:** see notes 5 and 7.

### 2. The narrator never changes, and in places he breaks his own rules (all episodes; 2, 3, 7, 8 on guardrails)

**Evidence:** the reel has 141 V.O. lines, 11–12 in every episode, against a budget of 5–8. The Ep1 script has 5; the Ep1 reel has 12.
- **One shape.** Almost every line is a denial or a shrink that the picture then catches: "i wasn't in the line. i was near it." / "i wasn't watching the hill. i was testing a pen." / "i didn't watch him leave. i was getting water."
- **Near-repeats:**
  - "we paused. i want to be very clear that we paused." (9.16) and "we all paused. i was very clear about that." (10.25)
  - "on the record" (7.33, 8.02, 12.09)
  - "it didn't bother me" (3.23, 7.12)
- **The voice's planned arc ([POV §10](../bible/pov-and-framing.md#10-the-pov-across-the-season-proposed)) isn't in the reel:**
  - The hug should play in V.O. silence, but 7.33 and 7.34 both carry lines.
  - Ep8 should have no V.O. in court, but 8.02, 8.15, 8.31 and 8.33 do.
  - Eps 10–11 should thin and shift to the present tense, but the line count doesn't move.
  - Ep12 has 12 lines.
- **Lines that cross the POV guardrails ([§2.1](../bible/pov-and-framing.md#21-the-principle-wrong-in-tone-never-in-fact), [§2.3](../bible/pov-and-framing.md#23-the-devices)):**
  - 2.27, "i didn't know.": restates his real line and claims what he knew at a real event.
  - 3.41, "i gave to a fund. he turned around. those are two separate things.": a caught device beside money at a real event. When the picture corrects him, the edit asserts a link between the donation and the podium turning.
  - 7.34, "i don't know what the other arm was doing.": claims what he knew at a real signing.
  - 8.02, 8.31 and 8.33 put spin beside a credibility question in court.

**Fix:** cut each reel to its budget and give the voice an arc:

| Eps | What the V.O. does |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Small words, one catch each. The pilot's five lines are the model. |
| 4–6 | The spin grows longer as he has more to hide. |
| 7 | Silence at the hug. The capital I is the only comment. |
| 8 | Nothing in court. The courtroom buffers. |
| 9 | He catches up to today. |
| 10–11 | Present tense and fewer lines. The machine starts finishing his sentences (11.05 already does this on screen). |
| 12 | Three lines at most, ending on "second time." |

Also restore the canonical "i don't keep score." at 1.31, where the reel has "count."

### 3. The finale pays off every gag except what Mas wants (Eps 1, 12)

**Evidence:** [mas-manalt.md](../characters/mas-manalt.md) gives Ep12's want as "to be fired, or at least asked," planted by Ep1's D8 line, "gerg never waits to be asked."
- Neither line is in the reel. 1.38 plays the Gerg beat without it, and the Ep12 outline and beats don't carry the payoff either.
- The vote (12.22) is a hands-up wide shot, and the veto (12.24) is Mas facing the monitor.
- Gerg, Mas's one loyalist, has no finale beat.

**Fix:**
- **Ep1:** put D8 back at 1.38, where the exchanged look with Gerg is already staged.
- **Ep12:** make the vote the payoff.
  - Every hand goes up except Gerg's.
  - Gerg is the one person who turns and asks Mas what he wants.
  - The model's veto lands before Mas can answer.
  - The three empty bubbles at 12.40 then read as the first question of the season that nothing answers for him.

It's an invented beat with no real event near it.

### 4. Ep12 peaks at 53% and then waits four minutes for its ending

**Evidence:**
- **Timing:** the veto lands at 11:41 and PEON AI at 13:01. Act Three (12.28–12.34, 14:30–18:30) is seven beats of endings montage before 1993 arrives at 19:30: KORG, Nole's appeal, Q\*, Mario's summary, EMIT, the critics, THE BIDDER.
- **Against the template:** the pacing model puts the S3 climax at 65–85% and flagged Ep12 as the episode missing THE PLAN. The reel still has none.
- **Two machine faces:** the machine appears twice at the table, as THE INTERN with the label gun (12.10) and as THE MODEL on the monitor (12.07–12.39). The reel never says which one is running the dinner.

**Fix:**
- **Montage:** move the endings montage under the credits, where the KORG 5 stinger already sits. Let the veto run straight into the class photo, then 1993, then `define "win."`, then the ring.
- **THE PLAN:** reprise Ep1's, `HOW TO FIRE A CEO WHO OWNS NOTHING.`
  - The humans redraw it at the table, and steps 1–3 tick.
  - Step 4, blank in the pilot, fills itself in: `VETO.`
  - This bookends the pilot and puts the humans' plan at the climax.
  - It also replaces the proposed kill-switch PLAN, which would be a sixth unpressed button (note 6).
- **Runtime:** the four freed minutes go to the dinner (THE PLAN, the vote, F12.1). The pacing model already says the dinner has to carry 10–12 minutes.
- **One machine face:** either the Intern hands the label gun to the monitor, or the monitor wears the Intern's lanyard as its successor.

### 5. Ep11 is built backwards

**Evidence:**
- **Late heist:** the A-plot in the logline, the last human heist, starts at 12:27 (11.27).
- **Mas is absent:** the first twelve minutes are the Researcher alone (the office, the loss curve, the sirens, the call to RUMPT, the label cartridge, SI FORCE), mostly without Mas. At 26%, it's the lowest Mas share of the season.
- **An interrupted climax:** the episode's best beat, "it thinks i'm part of it" (11.36), is followed by Alyi's door (11.37) and a 40 s flashback, THE DIFF (11.38–11.39), before the cliff at 11.40.
- **An overloaded PLAN:** it carries two concepts, the successor loop and then the assist clause (11.16–11.17), and runs straight into the Charter flashback (11.18), which breaks L6.

**Fix:**
- **Cold open:** open in the dark room, with Mas watching the Researcher's office on his monitor. The machine grows inside his view.
- **Crew:** assemble the crew by the end of Act One by moving 11.27–11.28 up. The heist then runs through Acts Two and Three, with the politeness loop as the obstacle it has to cross.
- **THE DIFF:** move it to Act One as the trigger for "our": the Researcher opens `mas_manalt.html` while it builds its successor. The heist then runs clean from 11.36 to the switch.
- **THE PLAN:** give it one concept, the assist clause. The nested lanyards (11.07) already explain the successor loop.
- **The Charter:** play Gerg's 2 a.m. typing before THE PLAN, as its trigger.

### 6. The endgame repeats three beats (Eps 9–12)

**Evidence:**
- **Pace:** Ep9 carries the word (9.12, 9.15, 9.16, 9.25, 9.26), and Ep10 carries the deal. The two halves overlap.
- **The horseshoe:** SREDNAS and NONNAB agree on every word and fight about the room four episodes running (9.27, 10.34, 11.24, 12.20). The gag matrix marks it paid off twice, in 11 and 12.
- **The button nobody presses:** it plays five times in two episodes: ISS's pause button (11.37), the switch on its pedestal (11.40–11.42), and the centerpiece three times over (12.15, 12.19, 12.20). The kill switches were already set up at 9.14 and 10.33.

**Fix:**
- **Pace:** Ep9 owns pace as a word and Ep10 owns it as a deal. Cut 10.25's repeat of 9.16's line.
- **Horseshoe:** keep it at 9.27 (setup) and 12.20 (payoff). Cut 10.34 and 11.24.
- **The button:** cut 11.37's version. Alyi's door can open on an empty white room instead: the man who left for safety has already left. Ep12 plays the standoff once, in three beats, with a turn the season hasn't done yet.

### 7. Ep5 is the weakest episode of the season

**Evidence:**
- **Cast and POV:** it has the most character ids (47), no two-shot of Mas with another person, and a Mas share of 38%.
- **Early resolution:** the A-plot (draft night against the monks' vigil) is finished by the midpoint at 11:45.
- **A flashback block:** seven flashback beats run back to back (5.20–5.27, 9:13–11:10) straight into THE PLAN, which breaks L6. F5.1 and F5.2 sit one minute apart. On the sheet this shows as a solid band of 16-colour stills in the middle of the episode.
- **An unrelated button:** YNOJ's cloth (5.44) has nothing to do with the A-plot.

**Fix:**
- **Split the block.** F5.1, the ramen coronation, moves up to Kram's $100M check in Act One; 5.19's `cost of my first draft: $200k` is already its trigger. F5.2, Alyi and THE WOODROSE part 2, stays at ISS's door, at least 3 minutes later.
- **Give Act Three to Mas.** Make GTP-5's launch its anchor: the monks chant under the launch, the chart is wrong, the router faints, and the episode ends on a Mas tell.
- **Compress the macro.** Fold the Senate's 99–1, the eraser and KORG (5.30–5.32) into one run of 45 s or less.
- **Move the cloth** to Ep12's endings montage, where the velvet-cloth gag (G32) is waiting for a payoff.

### 8. Eps 8–10 hand the story over to the news

**Evidence:**
- **Ep8:** after the verdict at 14:15, Act Three is nine macro beats (8.34–8.42): JERDNA, the Fed chair, THE FLIP, the EO coupon, the rocket, the G7, the podcast and the PACs. THE FLIP (8.36–8.37) makes Mas number two for the first time, the biggest turn in his arc since the Blip, and it's buried in the middle of that run.
- **Ep9:** the sandbox agents carry Act One and then vanish. Act Two (9.16–9.32) is 17 beats of mostly real items, and 86% of the episode's beats carry a real-event tag.
- **Ep10:** the A-plot's reveal, the Intern dealing (10.31), lands at 62%. Act Three is then a run of speculative gags: the evaluator, the kill-switch survey, the hearing, the Grand Prix and the bell.

**Fix:**
- **Ep8:** THE FLIP becomes the Act Three anchor: a close on Mas at number two, with the lighthouse outgrowing the cathedral behind him. Everything else goes into one run.
- **Ep9:** keep the agents in frame through Act Two as a GLYPH layer, `outside intended scope` in every tower that keeps building. Cut the horseshoe week and Dumfries down to one run.
- **Ep10:** push the dealer reveal to 70–75%, and make the cracked bell (10.36) its consequence. Cut the Grand Prix or the survey.

### 9. Relationships are carried by motifs, not scenes

**Evidence:** only 22 of the season's 530 beats frame Mas with exactly one other human character, and Eps 5, 9 and 12 have none. The relationships repeat rather than develop:
- **Alyi** is "only a reflection" in six episodes (2, 5, 6, 8, 11, 12), and the device never changes.
- **Nole** is angry at a calm Mas in every scene. The relationship does something new only at the dinner, when he flees Mas's side (12.07).
- **Misanthropic** gets the same roast every week: Mario's `Addendum:` (7 beats) and Adelina's "In plain English" (10 beats across 9 episodes).

**Fix:**
- **One quiet two-hander per episode** for the relationship that episode is about. Make it invented and keep it away from real events. For example:
  - Mas and Nole on Market Street after the tumbleweed (Ep4), or across the calendar after the verdict (Ep8)
  - Mas and Mario on the bell rope (Ep10)
  - Mas and Gerg at the dinner (note 3)
- **Alyi:** give the reflection one change by the finale. Either he's physically in the room for a single beat, or his chair is empty and there's no reflection in it at all.
- **"In plain English":** rest it through Eps 5–9 so the Ep12 payoff ("it already did the plain English") lands.

### 10. Eps 2 and 3 run on the same engine

**Evidence:** the middle of both episodes is people leaving.
- **Ep2:** Alyi (2.20–2.21), EKIEL and the safety team (2.22), and the NDA (2.26–2.28).
- **Ep3:** Gerg's sabbatical (3.05), LUNCHMAS (3.06, 3.24–3.27), the revolving door and Rima (3.14–3.16), and at THE WOODROSE the chairs of everyone who later left freezing (3.18).

Ep3's logline also juggles four threads.

**Fix:**
- **Ep2 owns the departure,** and Alyi is the loss.
- **Ep3's A-plot is the price and the hidden thinking:** the strawberry, THE PLAN, the `$157B` odometer and the podium's coin slot.
- **The revolving door becomes the turbine that spins the odometer.** 3.16 is already halfway there.
- **WOODROSE part 1 answers only its own question:** *who was at the table?*

## Is the satire still even-handed?

**Mostly, yes.**
- Every real-events episode pairs its RUMPT beats with a Democratic one: NEDIB's deepfakes, REMUHCS's forums, ISOLEP, MOSWEN in Eps 3, 5, 6 and 9, RENEIW's hydra, SIRRAH's blocks.
- The Orb fact-checks both sides (2.42, 5.43), and prop parity holds.
- Misanthropic is roasted in every episode, and the endgame roasts everyone at the table.

There are three soft spots:
- **Ep7 is the thinnest on weight.**
  - The administration gets the cold open, Davos, the ultimatum, the Rubicon, a 7-second meteor, the stamp and the council photo.
  - The other side gets the `STRONGLY WORDED` receipt (7.32) and a caption mention of NERRAW's letter (7.35).
  - [Guardrail 2a.3](../bible/guardrails.md#2a-the-rules) asks for a verified beat of similar weight. Promote NERRAW's Jan 29 backstop question to Mas (master timeline, [H]) to an on-camera beat that brings back Ep6's backstop, or give the receipt a set-piece of its own.
- **3.41's V.O.** (note 2) turns the coin-slot metaphor into the show asserting a real quid pro quo. Cut the line and let the clink stay a picture.
- **Ep7's REAL strip prints real names on screen.** About 30 of its 44 beats show "Trump", "Anthropic", "Altman", "Hegseth", "OpenAI", "Sora" and others; see the frame at 7.31. Every other episode uses parody names in its tags. Re-tag Ep7 from [naming.md](../bible/naming.md) and re-render it before the reel leaves the room.

## World stakes (for the season story revision)

> **Head writer, 2026-09-25.** The showrunner asked how much the season carries China, public sentiment, job loss and the wider geopolitical and economic interplay. The measured answer: very little lands on anyone.
> - China is scenery. Mas has one China line in twelve episodes.
> - The public is nearly gone after Ep2.
> - No ordinary worker is ever on screen.
> - The economy is mostly numbers on props.
>
> The full plan, with sources, tags, arcs and guardrails, is in [bible/world-stakes.md](../bible/world-stakes.md). This section lists the insertions for the revision to apply. **No episode file has been edited yet.**

**The rules for every insertion:**
