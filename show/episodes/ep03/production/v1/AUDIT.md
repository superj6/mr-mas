## 2. Conflicts and concrete fixes

### 2.1 Agency and the inner voice — W1, W5–9

The draft gives Mas a strong recurring want (“how long?”) but its answer rarely changes what he does. The new chain uses public actions and openly absurd logistical decisions:

- Act One: choose public teaser; ask whether the preview can answer a concrete request; choose preview over delaying the whole product; publish the vision essay while making a release/compute brief for the team.
- Act Two: ask for a usable handover, redistribute work after the departure and negotiate money/availability. The departures are obstacles, never portrayed as results of his secret design.
- Act Three: choose a build cadence amid medals, the rival's public essay and the election; call Gerg about the key and retain the full-sized photo; leave the rival's counter-move enough time to work.
- Act Four: agree the twelve-day launch order, show it in action and enter the donor queue with a check already chosen. His money gets a clink; the much larger pledge drives the podium's response.

Sixteen inner thoughts follow these moves. At least half are practical goals; two or three give vulnerability/gap, one warmth and at most one prediction. No thought invents motive behind resignations, veto, firing, lawsuit or election. The final “Say a number” lands without explanatory V.O.

### 2.2 One correct ML scene — W22

Maon has to make Mas understand why a plausible quick answer is insufficient before the product demonstration. The sentence on his monitor becomes a 2017 paper/prototype for about 28 s, motivated by Mas's question. In the next-token visual each new token attends only to the earlier context; candidate bars change and one token appends, then the loop repeats. Attention links are learned weights, not literal thought-reading or a truth checker. “Next word” is useful speech shorthand; the visual calls the small pieces tokens.

Draft lines [INVENTED]:

> MAS: “why does it sound so sure?”
>
> MAON: “It's choosing the next piece of text. Here, look. ‘The trophy didn't fit in the suitcase because it was too…’ It can put more weight on ‘trophy’ and favor ‘big’. Change the last word to ‘small’ and the suitcase matters more. That's attention. It learns which earlier pieces matter for the next one.”
>
> MAS: “and then?”
>
> MAON: “The new piece goes on the end. Same job again. A convincing sentence can still be wrong.”

For a 28 s limit, tighten spoken wording and let the two versions play as diagrams rather than read every label. The original transformer paper includes encoder/decoder attention; the decoder-like language-model graphic deliberately masks future tokens. Do not assert that every current GTP sees every future word.

The stadium can show hesitation, a hidden process and a correct answer without teaching test-time compute. Pro can be sold as the higher-priced tier without repeating the mechanism. This preserves the season ladder.

### 2.3 Gerg's motive flashback — W10–12

Woodrose's current dimming list shows loss, not a choice. Keep the table and its place cards, but let a small conversation happen before the chairs dim. A phone runs the Atari Breakout agent (two seconds, no self-play explanation). Gerg wants a real thing to build; the obstacle is an AGI discussion with no clear first task or start date. Mas slides a blank sheet/napkin over, asks what can run Monday, and Gerg writes the first task instead of another employer's name. They choose to begin. Later desert/dimming shots say only who is absent, never why anyone left. Return on a candle becoming the beach laptop key, then Mas's “and your chair?” and Gerg's living answer.

Planned memory budget: Woodrose 65 + transformer 28 + TPOOL 15 + exodus 30 = 138 s [J], within the 150 s guide. Actual lock supersedes this estimate.

### 2.7 Picture, sound and runtime

The screenplay must supply changing action under holds: hands, gaze, listener, interrupting work, moved prop. Room tone always has a route; score holds a sequence rather than fragments under each line. The show keeps knee/chip/no-third/96 BPM identity while moving from caper to warmth to spectacle to political discomfort. Large stadium and Shipmas passages can carry extended action, but there is no reason to pad ordinary conversations with seconds of frozen air.

A new pixel room should not be labeled “finished art” just because code renders. Sample every shot full size and inspect poses, contact, speaker, facial warmth, typography and read time. Paper pop-up leap retains its native medium contrast; generators remain optional replacements after the programmatic pass.
