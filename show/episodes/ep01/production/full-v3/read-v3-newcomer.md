# Ep1 v3: newcomer cold read

**Reader:** a newcomer who knows almost nothing about the AI industry or its real events.
**Watched:** `out/ep01/full-v3/ep01-v3.mp4` (Kokoro film, 21:26), as about one frame per second plus extra frames at the scene changes, read against the segment SRTs mapped onto the film clock. I read no script, beat plan, caption list or production doc before writing this.
**Date:** 2026-09-27.
**Timecodes:** all on the film clock (m:ss). The cold open is the old 30.67 s cut, and I ignored its last 4 s.

## 1. Retell (what I think happened)

1. Mas Manalt is the quiet, hoodie-wearing CEO of NopeAI, a San Francisco AI lab. He talks in lowercase, and he narrates his own inner monologue in dry one-liners.
2. On Nov 30, 2022, he lets his co-founder Gerg ship a "low-key research preview" chatbot (ChatGTP) over Rima's objections. It flatters everyone, reaches a million users in days, and the server bill explodes.
3. Rival Elgoog panics (a "code red"). Its retired founders come back, and it turns out Elgoog invented the tech NopeAI used.
4. Macrosoft's boss Tasya, "the landlord", invests billions and gives NopeAI free servers. Rent is "due on the first." I read the collar as Mas now being owned.
5. Macrosoft puts NopeAI's model into its search engine. Elgoog's rushed demo gets a telescope fact wrong and loses $100B in a day. Mas: "ours does that too."
6. Mario, who used to work at NopeAI, left to build a "careful" rival. Others sign an open letter asking every lab to pause, and one signer secretly builds his own.
7. Through 2023, Mas tours Washington and the world asking to be regulated: a White House photo, a Senate hearing where he says he owns no equity, an extinction statement. Meanwhile the chip seller Invidia gets rich.
8. By autumn Mas is on top. An eye-scanning orb verifies he is human, he trolls online that "AGI has been achieved internally," and he headlines DevDay next to his investor.
9. On Nov 17, 2023, in Las Vegas, four board members (three others had quit that year) kick him off a noon video call. Their post says he "was not consistently candid." Rima becomes interim CEO, and Gerg quits.
10. The internet floods them with hearts. The board offers the CEO job to Mario, who says no, then swaps Rima for a streamer, Ttemme, who wants to know why they fired Mas and apparently never finds out.
11. Macrosoft announces that Mas and Gerg will join it. 745 of 770 staff, including board member Alyi, who voted to fire Mas, sign a letter saying they will follow unless the board resigns.
12. The board falls apart, and on Nov 21 Mas returns as CEO with a new board under a chair dressed as a firefighter. There will be an "independent review" of an unnamed something.
13. He comes back to a "Days since someone tried to fire Mas: 0" sign and a safe marked "Q\* · DO NOT OPEN. DO NOT EXPLAIN." He unscrews Alyi's nameplate while posting that he has "zero ill will" toward him.
14. In the tag he is a magazine's CEO of the Year and gets hit with a newspaper's copyright lawsuit. He says "noted.", the same word he said at the start.

**What does Mas want?** I honestly can't say. In Act One he wants the launch to go well, and he wants to be liked ("it likes me," "i still read it twice"). In Acts Two and Three he wants nothing I can see: he gets carried from event to event. In Act Four he presumably wants his job back, but others win it for him. Gerg builds the fallback, the staff sign the letter and Tasya offers the desks. Mas's own actions are "super.", "leave it open." and clicking OK. That makes him a charming but **passive** lead, and it is the biggest reason the middle of the film felt like a history montage rather than a story.

## 3. Transitions that came out of nowhere

1. **3:35 NopeAI basement (Dec 5) → Elgoog campus, "CODE RED" (Dec 21).** *Missing:* who the two retired silhouettes are, and why they matter. The phone alert gives the "why now", but the scene lands on people we've never met. *Would help:* a one-line card for the pair, like Radnus got ("THE FOUNDERS · RETIRED 2019"), or a line such as "we invented this in 2017."
2. **5:27 Elgoog loses $100B → NopeAI GTP-4 split with Mario's lab.** Mario's only setup is the intro's name dump. The VO "he left to build a careful one" arrives *during* the scene, while three other gags compete with it. *Would help:* let the VO land on a clean shot of Mario's lab *before* the split and the memo business.
3. **6:03 → the pause letter.** A limbo room with no date place and none of our characters (Mas isn't there). *Missing:* why we're here and what it has to do with Mas. *Would help:* Mas reading the letter at his desk ("they want us to stop. we just started."), which would also motivate his "PLEASE."
4. **6:18 → PLEASE as the Act One out.** The word has no antecedent. *Would help:* show at least "PLEASE REG—" and cut, or have the letter at 6:03 end with Mas picking up a pen.
5. **7:41 White House class photo → the May 12 deepfake repost → the Senate.** *Missing:* where we are (whose room? whose phone?), who the orange-haired man is, and whose voice we hear. *Would help:* cut the Rumpt(?) repost entirely and go straight to the senator hearing his own cloned voice, with a label ("THE SENATOR'S VOICE · AI-GENERATED"). Keep one deepfake idea, not two.
6. **9:46 Invidia's trillion → the bucket-and-waterline image → Act Three.** *Missing:* whose head is in the water, and what the metaphor means. Nothing earlier uses water. *Would help:* either set up the image (Mas saying "we're underwater on compute" in Act One would do it) or replace it with something already established, like the users counter or the bill.
7. **9:52 Act Three opens on the Coinworld orb box.** *Missing:* why Mas is a co-founder of an eyeball-scanner, and why proving you're human matters. *Would help:* one line of VO: "my other company. it checks if you're a person. soon that'll be a hard question."
8. **12:00 Act Four opens in Las Vegas: "the race is tomorrow."** *Missing:* what race, and why he's there. Also, the board as antagonists: Neleh, Mada and the Quiet Vote have never been on screen before 12:08. *Would help:* in Act Two or Three, one beat of board friction. For example, Neleh publishing something critical of NopeAI and Mas being annoyed, or a board meeting where Mas "forgets" to mention the launch ("we found out about it on the internet"). That would also give the "not consistently candid" charge something concrete behind it. Right now the charge refers to nothing I saw.
9. **15:32: from "we fired him" to "we've talked all day about him coming back."** This is the biggest jump in the film. Between 14:41 (hearts) and 15:32 we see the board decide to write step four themselves and call Mario, which points *away* from Mas. Then suddenly they're negotiating his return. *Missing:* the pressure that flipped them (investors calling? staff threatening?) and the decision to talk. *Would help:* a 5–10 s beat of the board's phones lighting up with INVESTORS / STAFF, and Neleh saying "They want him back. We'll talk. We won't agree." Then the lobby footage lands.
10. **15:47 Ttemme appears.** *Missing:* who he is, and why Rima is out. *Would help:* a descriptor card like the others get (e.g. "TTEMME · INTERIM CEO #2 · STREAMER"), and one line explaining Rima's removal ("Rima wants him back.").
11. **16:10 Tasya walks into the NopeAI boardroom at 11:53 PM to read Macrosoft's statement.** It's staged to read as a twist, but it takes a second to parse that Macrosoft is hiring Mas away. The "MAS · GERG →" sign helps. It's acceptable as is.
12. **19:04 → 19:07: from "everyone's packed for Macrosoft" plus Tasya's threat, to "agreement in principle."** The staff letter and the board leaving the call (18:14–18:29) do set it up, so this one is mostly earned. The fires and the firefighter chair make it read. It's only abrupt because the *terms* are delivered as a read-out, not seen.
13. **20:12 → the Q\* safe.** It comes after the plot has resolved, and nothing points to it. Fine as a teaser for later, but it lands as a non-sequitur. If it's meant as the unspoken reason for the firing, plant it earlier: a locked door in Act Three that Alyi stares at.

Transitions that worked well: **5:14 "ACROSS TOWN, AT ELGOOG"** (a clear card, a clear joke). **11:50 the cold-open interview reappears on Mas's monitor, then the invite, then 11:59 PM** (a strong, clean bridge into Act Four). **12:08 the HOW TO FIRE A CEO blueprint** (it tells me exactly who and how). **18:14 the staff swarming the board's call.**

## 4. Lines and moments that felt unnatural or stilted

- **Real quotes read out as dialogue** (0:05, 5:05 "…made them dance…", 11:46 "We love you guys.", 14:19 "You can call it this way… I disagree with this.", 18:56 "…below them, above them, around them."). When a character speaks with quote marks and ellipses, it stops being a person talking. The 14:19 Alyi line in particular sounds like a translation. Where the quote is framed as a clip, post or statement (16:15, 20:24), it works. Where it's spoken face to face, it doesn't.
- **4:34–4:49 Tasya's rent speech** runs 15 s, the longest line in Act One, and it's exposition. The closing turn "Rent is due on the first." is great. The middle could lose half.
- **3:21 "You're actually crying for them."** The picture doesn't show crying, and "crying for them" is an odd phrase.
- **6:31 "What can be, unburdened by what has been… trained."** A reference joke that, for a newcomer, is just garbled.
- **"Gerg has never waited to be asked." (14:35) and "gerg never waits to be asked." (17:47)** Two nearly identical tag-lines three minutes apart, the second as VO. It reads as the writer's motto for Gerg rather than something either speaker would say.
- **The "compiling" motif** (10:5x "When it compiles.", 16:50 VO "he'll say he's compiling", 16:53, 17:52 "ask me when it compiles"). Cute once or twice. By the fourth time I could see it coming.
- **19:04 "Hello."** A flat line with no addressee.
- **17:35 "That'll be the share sale. Everybody's been waiting on that one."** Expository, and it assumes I know what a share sale is.
- **12:01 VO "the race is tomorrow."** It assumes knowledge I don't have.
- **15:05 "Then we'll write step four ourselves."** It sounds decisive, but the next scenes contradict it: they don't write it, events do.
- **Long static holds** where nothing on screen changes while people talk: 10:34–11:06 (about 30 s, Gerg off screen), 13:37–13:51 (14 s on the blog draft), 14:45–15:05 (20 s on a wide shot with unclear speakers), 15:32–15:46 (14 s on the security camera), 16:53–17:05 (the Gerg video). Each one alone is fine, but together they make Act Four feel like a radio play with slides.

## 6. The big-picture note (plain)

For about 12 minutes (Acts One to Three), this is a sharp, pretty sketch reel of 2023 history with Mas as our tour guide. The *story* (the board firing him) only starts at 11:50, and its antagonists are strangers when they strike. The cold open's calendar invite is the only thread that carries the board plot through the first half. Two things would fix most of my confusion:

- **Plant the board and the candour problem early.** One or two beats in Acts Two and Three where Mas is visibly less than candid *with the board specifically*. Put Neleh, Mada and the Quiet Vote in one earlier scene, and let Alyi's worry ("what if it wakes up?") grow into a visible rift.
- **Give Mas one active choice in Act Four** before the staff and Gerg win it for him. Right now his biggest decisions are "super.", "leave it open." and clicking OK.
