## 0. The short version

- **What changed from v4.** v4 was 45 lines and 176 words in 4:11, a median of 4 words a line, and its longest conversation ran 10 s. Draft 5.1 is **101 voiced lines and 836 words**, plus 7 silent posts, with a median of 5 words a line. 27 lines are three words or fewer, and most of those are Mas's or the kept seasoning ("When?", "Will we?", "More. Soon.", "Good question." twice, "…Ah.", "okay."). Mas has 23 lines (median 3 words, every one complete, about 12% of the act's words), because the people pressing him now talk in full.
- **Conversations, not bursts.** The act's talk is fourteen conversations. The long ones:
  - the committee running into the call to the rival lab (~69 s)
  - Sunday: the failed talks, the second interim CEO, Tasya's statement (~79 s)
  - Gerg at 2 AM with the letter (~62 s)
  - step two and Rima (~47 s)
  - the terms (~40 s)
- **Covered so they can play.** Every conversation is carried by one held setup (a two-shot, an over-the-shoulder, the call grid, the split), with cuts only on real turns: new information, a reaction that matters more than the speaker, or new geography. The act has **81 shots** in about 9:36 (the avalanche's five counted separately). The average shot is about 7.1 s, and no talk scene cuts per line (§3).
- **Seasoning, rare.** Two dashes ("hypothetically—", "char—"), each after the thought has landed, and one designed overlap (Adelina in over Mario's last syllable). "super." no longer plays under the board's reading of its post.
- **No blurts.** Every new voice has a lead-in (a ring, a chime, a door, a spotlight, a hand up), and every line has a listener and a trigger the audience has just seen or heard (§3.1 names each).
- **Music.** One continuous performance per sequence, re-rendered to these lengths. Under talk it **thins to its pedal and ducks**; it comes up between conversations. There are three dead stops (D6's digital silence at the Cancel click, Mada's label, "of what?"), one ring-out, and a few designed rests (§4).
- **Runtime, planned: about 9:36** (12:31–22:07). Like for like, the new talk adds about 42 s of speech to 5.0's; the rest of the difference from 5.0's 7:59 is that this plan gives every pause its written length (§6). **Pass one is about 4:21, some 45% of the act**, which is a ruling for the showrunner.
- **Build order (showrunner note 1).** Record the takes from §7, then a **stick-figure reel** (the real takes, a temp bed, stick figures and text cards) for the showrunner to approve flow and dialogue. The pixel animatic comes only after that approval (§8).

**Mix guides** (from [voice-diagnosis-v4 §4.6](voice-diagnosis-v4.md) and the v4 sound pass; guides, not gates):
- Pre-duck the score 150–300 ms ahead of a line, with a 150–250 ms attack, and release it over 400–800 ms. Hold the duck across gaps under about 2.5 s inside an exchange, so it doesn't pump.
- Under a **silent post**: thin the melody, but hold the rest at level (about a −3 dB duck, with +2 dB on the held families inside the thin).
- Under a **voiced real line**: thinned in the render (no melodic onsets) and ducked as for talk. No comic scoring, no stinger, no melody on the line.
- **Stops:** three dead stops (D6, Mada's label, "of what?"), each on a story beat with room tone under it, each with a clear re-entry (a new phrase or a pre-lap), never a half-second blip. **One ring-out** (Gerg's look).
- **Designed rests:** the four dial tones, the sand's beat, and the silence after "Any objections?" (pedal and tick hold; not a stop).

## 7. The full line list, for the recording

**101 voiced lines, 836 words**, plus 7 silent posts (listed where they land, marked "post"; they are never voiced). IDs are v5's own (`a5-<scene>-<nn>`). The word count is taken by the same script for every draft. The v4 dialogue doc counts differently, so compare within one method.

**The method** ([voice-diagnosis-v4 §4](voice-diagnosis-v4.md#4-the-recording-method-for-v5); guides, with the ear deciding):
- **One whole read per turn.** Split only at a real change of thought, with a breath there.
- **Speed from intent, within the character's band,** held within a scene (±0.05) unless the scene changes their state. Kokoro speed stays within about 0.85–1.12. **No time-stretch, ever.** Cut the picture to the take.
- **Pauses** inside a turn are opened with room tone at the quietest 5 ms of the existing gap, with 8 ms crossfades. Use 0.25–0.45 s between sentences of one thought and 0.5–0.9 s for a change of thought, and never two pauses of the same length in a row. The "Delivery" column gives each one.
- **Breaths** go before the first line of an exchange, after a second or more of silence, and before any turn of three or more sentences. Use generic licensed breaths, never one taken from or matched to a real person.
- **Heads and tails:** at least 0.12–0.15 s before the first sound, the natural tail down to −60 dB plus 0.1 s, and 0.3–0.5 s room handles each side.
- **Real lines are read as finished sentences.** An ellipsis is a print mark for the trim, never a trail in the voice.
- **The two interrupted lines are recorded complete.** Mario: "…under which we might, hypothetically, consider it." Neleh: "Has anyone read the charter?" The interrupter comes in over the last syllable (Adelina), or the world takes the tail (the tile's exit); the trailing voice drops −6 to −10 dB and goes out over 100–200 ms.
- **Questions Kokoro may not lift.** Try rewording first (logged as a text change), then two or three seeds and speeds within the band, and a gentle final-syllable bend only as a last resort, flagged for the ear. The lines: "Do you have any questions?", "Is his feed frozen?", "Any objections?", "For how long?", "What should I tell them?", "Will we?", "When?", "You've got the staff letter open?", "Do I tell everyone to pack?", "you're staying?", "Is it ready?", "of what?", "Chat… for how long?". Most carry the question in their words already.

  - GERG "gerg" (hard G)
  - ALYI "AL-yee"
  - NELEH "NEL-eh"

## 8. Build order: stick figures first (showrunner note 1)

"we should've been iterating on cheaper stick figure runs to nail down flow and dialogue before final render". So v5 is built in this order, and nothing pixel-final is drawn until step 4 is approved.

1. **Record** all 101 lines by §7, in scene order, one whole read per turn. Keep every alternate take.
2. **Ear pass on the takes** by a person, scene by scene in context, before anything is laid to picture. It checks whether a line sounds like someone talking, whether the questions lift, and whether the real lines land as finished sentences. Then the numeric QA of voice-diagnosis §4.7 (paired UTMOS, CER, pace stability) and its hard checks (no stretch, no splice, no digital black, heads, tails and handles).
3. **A stick-figure reel of the whole act**:
   - the real takes, placed with §7's gaps
   - the temp bed: the room tones, plus each sequence's cue as one continuous temp performance, even if it's only the pedal and the tick
   - stick figures or simple silhouettes in the planned setups, and cards for the text items (the rails, plates, posts, the letter, the check, the signs), each held for its read
   - the planned shot changes from §3.2, and nothing more

   It's cheap to redo, so it can go round several times.
4. **The showrunner watches the reel and approves flow and dialogue.** Every change there is made in the script and §7 first, then re-recorded or re-cut.
5. **Only then** the v5 lock and the pixel animatic: `shots5` / `data-v5` / `sound-v5` and a `lock_v5`, built from the approved reel's frames, with kept shots keeping their v4 numbers. The cues get re-rendered to the lock, the report tools get re-run, and new newcomer and insider reads are done on the pixel cut.

The v4 files stay untouched for comparison.

## 10. Resources that would speed this up (for the lead to ask the showrunner)

The showrunner asked to be told when more resources would help. These would, most to least:

5. **Disk.** About 7 GB is free (the disk is 99% full). A full reel plus a v5 render and mixes will be tight.
